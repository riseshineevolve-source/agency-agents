# The Midnight Carnival Below the City

World C · Optical Halloween · Commission dated 2026-10-08

**Status: CONCEPT_ONLY · IMAGE_NOT_GENERATED · PRINT_NOT_PROVEN.** This is a story bible and illustration concept bank, not owner-approved final art. Candidate rankings are editorial judgments made through the requested specialist lenses; no separate specialist agents, generated images, printed proofs, or independent approvals are represented.

## Elevator pitch

An unaccompanied traveler accepts a ticket made from reflected tram light and descends into a subterranean carnival whose final applause has stopped one beat short. Each attraction teaches her a different part of performance until she discovers that nobody stole the ending: a performer and an audience are waiting for each other to begin. She must stop watching from safety, offer the missing beat, and help strangers return music to a city approaching dawn.

The visual signature is a working carnival turned into severe, beautiful optical architecture: immense ink-black machinery, white landing surfaces, bare human hands, hinged velvet, and precisely situated reflections. This world is about timing and public participation. Its spaces should feel inhabited by skilled people, not populated by decorative monsters.

## Protagonist, cast, and stakes

**Mara, the traveler:** an adult woman with a straight jaw, short blunt hair, a calf-length black coat with one broad white lapel on her left, plain trousers, and flat shoes. Her hands are bare. Her habit is to wait until she understands a situation before entering it; this caution initially protects her at the glass lift, then becomes the very habit preventing the finale. She changes by offering a modest footfall and an open hand before anyone guarantees the result. Her left lapel remains on her anatomical left in direct views; reflections transform it according to their surfaces.

**Neri, the stagehand:** an adult with close-cut hair, a plain black work vest, broad white sleeves, and a visible, practical grip on rails or mechanisms. Neri demonstrates safe contact, supplies the organ diagnosis, and helps change the audience route. Neri cannot supply the unbound fourth count: as part of the carnival, their completed performance actions are caught in its missing interval.

**Vale, the aerial performer:** an adult in a fitted black performance suit with a single white square cuff. Vale landed safely but slightly outside the intended final pose and extended a hand toward the audience. The audience waited for a corrected finish; Vale waited for their response. This reciprocal hesitation is the cause of the lost beat, not a punishment for imperfection. Other attractions can include unnamed adult performers; they are not duplicate Vales.

**The ringmaster:** a distant caretaker in a long, angular formal coat, identifiable by a broad white collar and one white cuff. Appears only in **C-04, C-10, C-14, and C-18**, always a small figure. No close portrait, weapon, predatory grin, or villain reveal. The ringmaster holds a stage safety fold until the landing and fourth beat can agree.

**The audience:** ordinary adult strangers in severe evening clothes, with individual posture and clear hands. Their apparent passivity is uncertainty about when they are welcome to respond. Their decision to join matters as much as Mara's first beat.

**Stakes:** the carnival's finale cannot conclude, its organ cannot complete the musical phrase, and its departure route cannot open into morning. Mara's ticket borrows one unfinished interval; if she merely watches, she joins the waiting audience and her return remains suspended. Nobody is promised bodily harm. The dramatic cost is becoming an observer who cannot leave an experience unfinished.

## Physical, temporal, and visual rules

1. **One optical governing rule in each image.** Supporting perspective, ordinary shadows, and material edges must serve that rule rather than create rival illusions. Every impossible-space contradiction is localized and named; the rest of the scene has ordinary support and scale.
2. **Time withholds completion, not thought or every movement.** People can walk, communicate, and demonstrate in provisional time. Attractions can rehearse their first three phases; their terminal fourth event stalls. The final landing, closing chord, applause, and exit cannot complete independently. No duplicated bodies or historical images are caused by this rule.
3. **The fourth beat requires an invitation accepted.** Mara is unbound because she arrived after the arrest. Her footfall can initiate the missing interval, but the audience must answer and Vale must complete the landing. A solitary early clap cannot substitute for this exchange; Neri and the ringmaster can support it but cannot finish it for everyone.
4. **The safety fold is one stage joint.** Its unreal spatial continuation is exposed at C-11, understood through ordinary mirror geometry at C-14, and brought into registration at C-18. It is not a second carnival, a portal network, or a repeating maze. A protected service cradle and handrail provide Mara's actual support near it.
5. **The ticket is borrowed light, not a collectible.** At C-01 one fantasy event condenses a lamp reflection into a thin white rectangular ticket with a clipped corner. Its missing corner signifies the reserved final interval. It has no words, digits, icons to decipher, or required hidden copies. At C-20 it returns to actual reflected daylight when the interval has ended.
6. **Mirrors reflect present, existing subjects.** A plane mirror places virtual objects behind its plane; a floor mirror reflects only above-floor objects visible to it; fractured facets have independently plausible normals; the roof mirror uses two reflections. Do not manufacture independent poses, a hidden world below a floor, or arbitrary left/right flips. The light ticket's condensation does not license inaccurate reflection elsewhere.
7. **A glass slab displaces transmitted sightlines without moving the body.** A lamp produces cast shadows away from its source onto identified receiving planes. Anamorphic marks have one designed station point. Tessellated parts touch through an explicit repeating join. Optical pattern follows the form or its perspective.
8. **Halloween is material and theatrical.** Use bat-wing iron supports, thorn-cut hardware, mourning velvet, severe pointed hems, angular midnight costumes, articulated shadow theater, and after-hours city machinery. No pumpkins, sheet ghosts, graveyards, creepy clowns, masks, cute mascot vehicles, stock-vector Halloween clusters, or generic mandalas.
9. **Gallery art leads; coloring space is intentional.** Compose for 8.5 × 11 inch portrait, with a flexible approximately 40–55% solid near-black target. White faces, garment panels, landing planes, glass interiors, and equipment surfaces should remain broad and usable, with connected areas rather than scattered pinholes. Crisp ink contours; no gray gradients, fuzzy fur, tiny texture noise, pasted swirls, text, signature, or decorative border.
10. **No collection or production mechanics.** These twenty scenes are illustrations and concepts only. No search-token inventory, masks, seek-find requirements, assembly instructions, pagination plan, KDP workflow, or changes to Optical Animals.

## Five-act causal plot

**Act I — Threshold and ticket, 01–04:** the lamp reflection becomes an invitation; its clipped edge leads Mara to a viewpoint-dependent stair sign. She descends, discovers that glass can misrepresent footing, and reaches the finale's visible absence. An empty audience place makes the invitation personal. The organ's incomplete gesture sends her toward rehearsal attractions.

**Act II — Rules and kinetic attractions, 05–08:** stage stripes teach a pulse; a single mechanical carousel teaches carried momentum; an acrobat's floor reflection clarifies the real landing; suspended ropes require Mara to support a load instead of watching it. Her intervention lifts a lighting shutter, revealing the next problem.

**Act III — Freeze, false villain, and trap, 09–12:** a puppet shadow shows a fourth receiving plane left dark. Faceted reflections make the distant ringmaster seem to possess the withheld ending. Mara reaches toward the safety shutter through the trapeze service cradle and becomes caught in its folded waiting space. An early solitary clap in the applause hall returns out of phase. Listening and using a real maintenance rail bring her to the organ.

**Act IV — Cause and plan, 13–16:** an intact final organ key disproves mechanical sabotage. Neri reenacts the waiting hands, revealing that audience and performer each expected the other to finish first. A two-plane mirror establishes that the ringmaster is bracing the landing joint. Mara unfolds the velvet partition so spectators can meet the performer, then uses grounded ferris ribs to rehearse a shared pace. The plan is invitation, footfall, response, landing, music—not collecting a lost object.

**Act V — Collective resolution and dawn, 17–20:** Mara invites an audience member into the open lane; a reciprocal gesture draws others into the rhythm. Her fourth footfall begins the final interval, their hands answer, and Vale finishes while the stage joint registers. Neri releases the organ's completed phrase; its expanded bellows make restored time visible. Mara returns the borrowed light and leaves through the dawn-lit tram platform, carrying the habit of participation rather than a souvenir.

### 01 · C-01 · The Fare the Lamp Remembers

**Subject and Halloween signal:** Mara's bare hand, a tilted mirror-steel kiosk plate, and a deserted underground tram platform; the kiosk support has one broad bat-wing profile cut from iron, not a decorative flock.

**Incident → clue → action → result:** Waiting for a tram that does not arrive, Mara sees a rectangular lamp reflected in the kiosk plate. A physical bracket interrupts one corner of the lamp aperture, giving its reflection the shape of an incomplete ticket. She touches the reflected rectangle; the light condenses into a thin ticket at her fingertips. Its clipped corner points toward the mouth of a service stair. She accepts an invitation rather than searching for an object, and approaches that stair in C-02. Her decision initiates the plot; nothing has yet identified a culprit or promised a reward.

**Governing optical rule:** One planar plate reflects the actual visible lamp. Block source, plate normal, camera, and hand first; the virtual lamp belongs behind the plate. Light condensation is the stated fantasy event, not a second mirror law.

**Camera and hierarchy:** Low three-quarter view beside the kiosk: largest white shape is the lifted ticket and hand; second is Mara's angular sleeve; a small actual lamp establishes the reflection's source; the distant stair stays subordinate.

**Ink and colorable geometry:** Target about 47% black. Use broad kiosk faces, a clear white palm, one roomy lapel, and an uninterrupted lamp/ticket interior. Exclude shiny gray rendering; a white specular silhouette bounded by solid ink establishes polished steel.

**Secondary discovery:** The clipped corner is caused by the real lamp bracket, planting that meaningful absence can have a visible cause.

**QA concern:** Resolve all five fingers and ticket thickness; do not reflect the newly lifted ticket as though it were still flush with the plate. Check the source bracket's actual transformed position in the reflection.
