## Purpose

Compress the already-recovered brand, revenue-engine and website direction into one decision surface. This packet does **not** reopen strategy, invent products, change pricing, or authorize implementation.

- Audience gateways: **Kids & Families / Teens / Adults / Seniors**.
- The Happy Makers are the primary character IP for Kids & Families, not a mandatory visual layer for every audience.

- Primary v1 product focus: Happy Me / Happy Makers ecosystem.

Family variant: **Family growth, made playable.**

## Owner decisions required before Website v2 / English master freeze

### D1 — Master homepage promise

Choose which line becomes the primary homepage promise:

- **A — Real-life skills, made playable.**
- **B — Family growth, made playable.**

Recommended default for the umbrella homepage: **A**. It supports Kids & Families now while remaining compatible with Teens, Adults and Seniors. B remains useful on family-specific surfaces.

### D2 — Revenue-engine comparison line

Decide whether the line **“More useful than a printable. More playful than a parenting course. Far lighter than therapy.”** may appear in public-facing website copy, or remains an internal positioning compass only.

Recommended default: **internal / campaign-testing language first**, not a universal master-brand tagline. The therapy comparison needs careful context and should not carry the brand alone.

### D3 — Homepage acquisition priority

Choose the primary homepage conversion hierarchy for v1:

- **A — useful free experience / gift first, products second**
- **B — product/app discovery first, free experience second**

Recommended default: **A**, because it matches the recovered Revenue Engine and creates a measurable path from discovery to lead to Google Play.

### D4 — Audience navigation prominence

Confirm the top-level audience gateways as:

**Kids & Families → Teens → Adults → Seniors**

Recommended default: **approve as-is**. It preserves the umbrella architecture and the already-decided Seniors placement without forcing Happy Makers onto non-kids products.
