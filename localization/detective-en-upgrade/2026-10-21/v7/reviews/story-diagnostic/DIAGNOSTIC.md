## What should become stronger

The story already has a sound spine: a recruit becomes a trusted team member while ordinary cases gradually reactivate an old training route. Its strongest twists are earned reversals of the reader's interpretation, not surprise villains: a wrong photo attachment hides a signed return; a spoon label moved; two clocks disagree; two witnesses describe different sides of a panel move; an old note is Bibi's; the empty role belongs to the reader. Preserve those.

The remaining flatness comes mainly from prose packaging and exchange rhythm. Too many openers repeat: previous answer supplies one useful fact → staff encounter → last reliable placement → one room companion → next opener closes a mundane mix-up. The puzzles must retain that structure, but the narrative need not use the same explanation every time. Give each file its existing emotional or practical center: rehearsal clock, embarrassing hat, departing bus, wrongfully confident memory, the hush after a tear, tools finally delivered, maps the reader kept, an empty hook.

Do not manufacture higher stakes through theft, sabotage, danger, a watcher, new witnesses, or secrets the evidence cannot establish. Make an ordinary mystery feel immediate by showing the already-established consequence and giving one crisp unanswered question.

Too many Comms end with a mini-lesson after the joke has already landed. The method is printed in the task. Family dialogue can show it through behavior rather than repeat “facts first,” “start with evidence,” or “corrected time first.” In particular, remove the pressure for every final line to be a corrective adult/older-sibling instruction. Sometimes let Dilo or Alio land the last good line, let Mimi join the silliness, or let Luli make the joke rather than police it.

## Voice safeguards

- Mimi: capable captain, warm and practical, with a phone habit. She should make room for the reader and sometimes enjoy the nonsense. Avoid turning her into a sequence of parental commands.
- Luli: sharp, dry, specific; she notices exactly what others missed. Not the household correction machine. Affection is strongest when she joins a joke or quietly notices growth.
- Dilo: confident and technically competent, competitive with himself and objects. Preserve his actual wins and his willingness to cross out a beloved theory. Not a permanently wrong boy being supervised by girls.
- Alio: imaginative route/recon thinker with useful physical observations. His helmet and ambitious theories can be funny without making him foolish or helpless. His voluntary pause at Case24 is growth.
- Nini: naming, literal sideways insight, noticing people and room for their feelings. Not an omniscient oracle announcing the eventual map extraction early.
- Bibi: wry mentor, a former young detective with an excellent hat, honest about what memory does not prove. Neither a confused-age gag nor an archive oracle who secretly knew every answer all along.

All six need different sentence rhythms. Avoid a universal bureaucratic voice, “I shall” as every child's default, identical self-aware “I am learning” conclusions, or a roast pattern in which the same child always loses. Wry administrative humor is one family flavor, not every exchange's engine.
