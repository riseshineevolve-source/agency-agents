## Purpose

Translate useful buyer-psychology ideas from the owner-supplied Soul'd Out AI sales material into an original RSE marketing system that improves relevance, trust and conversion without manipulative dark patterns, fake urgency, unsupported neuroscience claims or copied proprietary naming.

This framework is for RSE positioning, product pages, KDP descriptions, social content, email, launch planning and AI prompting.

## Core rule

Do not ask AI merely to "write persuasive copy."

Before writing, diagnose:
1. who is buying,
2. what they want now,
3. what they hesitate about,
4. what evidence we actually have,
5. what real-life use they need to imagine,
6. why acting now is genuinely relevant,
7. what single next action we want.

The marketing job is to reduce uncertainty and make the value easy to picture, not to pressure people into a purchase they do not want.

## RSE Buyer-State Map

### State A — "I still want to make progress this year"

Typical season: September–October / back-to-routine / autumn reset.

Buyer psychology:
- renewed motivation,
- unfinished family goals,
- prior failed attempts,
- desire for something practical enough to use this week.

RSE response:
- make progress feel small, concrete and achievable;
- show immediate use, not abstract transformation;
- avoid guilt;
- emphasize one next move rather than a complete family overhaul.

Best-fit RSE products:
- Happy Makers Detective Academy,
- Happy Me,
- World 01 / World 02 later,
- practical family Gifts/Tiny Tools when promoted.

### State B — "Is this actually worth choosing?"

Typical season: Black Friday / deal-heavy comparison window.

Buyer psychology:
- many competing offers,
- discount skepticism,
- comparison fatigue,
- fear of buying another unused product.

RSE response:
- prove differentiated value before discussing discount;
- clarify exactly who the product is for / not for;
- demonstrate use cases, page/app experience and replay value;
- anchor value to what the buyer receives and uses, not inflated reference prices;
- never invent scarcity or fake markdowns.

### State C — "I want this purchase to mean something"

Typical season: November–December / gifting / holidays / fresh-start planning.

Buyer psychology:
- care,
- connection,
- identity,
- meaningful gifts,
- shared experiences,
- anticipation of January.

RSE response:
- make ownership vivid: where the book/app fits into family life;
- show the moment of giving/using/doing together;
- connect product to curiosity, confidence, connection or useful shared time;
- seasonal timing can be genuine when shipping, Christmas countdown or New Year use actually matters.

Best-fit RSE products:
- 24 Gentle Steps to Christmas,
- Detective Academy as a holiday gift/activity,
- Optical Animals as a creative gift,
- future family apps.

## RSE Purchase-Readiness Diagnosis

Before producing campaign copy, classify the main conversion blocker:

- DESIRE GAP: buyer does not yet care enough about the outcome.
- BELIEF GAP: buyer likes the outcome but doubts this product will deliver useful value.
- OWNERSHIP GAP: buyer cannot picture how the product fits real life.
- FRICTION GAP: buyer sees too much effort, complexity, setup or parental work.
- CHOICE GAP: buyer is confused about products/options.
- TIMING GAP: buyer sees no reason to act now.
- TRUST GAP: buyer lacks proof, transparency or confidence in RSE.

A campaign should solve the dominant blocker before adding urgency.

## Emotional Instruction Layer for AI

Every major RSE marketing prompt should specify:

### 1. Buyer
Who is reading and in what situation?

### 2. Current belief
What are they likely thinking before seeing the message?

### 3. Desired shift
What should they understand, feel or imagine next?

### 4. Evidence
What verified product facts support that shift?

### 5. Real-life scene
What concrete use or ownership moment should be easy to picture?

### 6. Hesitation
What reasonable objection should be answered?

### 7. Voice
RSE: warm, clever, practical, playful where audience-appropriate, zero parent guilt, no plastic AI language.

### 8. Action
One clear next step.

## Copy Quality Matrix

Score drafts internally from 0–2 on each dimension:

1. SELF-RELEVANCE
   - Does the buyer recognize their real situation?

2. CREDIBILITY
   - Are claims specific and supported?
   - Are expectations realistic?

3. OWNERSHIP / IMAGINABILITY
   - Can the buyer picture using, gifting or benefiting from the product?

4. DIFFERENTIATION
   - Is there a clear reason to choose this product instead of a generic printable/book/app?

5. FRICTION REDUCTION
   - Is it obvious how easy it is to start and use?

6. HESITATION HANDLING
   - Does the message answer the likely "yes, but..."?

7. TIMING
   - Is there a genuine reason this matters now?

8. VOICE
   - Does it sound human and specifically RSE, not generic AI copy?

9. ETHICS
   - No fake scarcity, fabricated proof, disguised manipulation, parent guilt, fear amplification or unsupported scientific/medical claims.

A draft should not ship because it sounds polished. It should ship when the buying questions are answered.

## Campaign sequence

For most RSE launches:

1. RECOGNITION — "this is my situation"
2. POSSIBILITY — "this could realistically help / be enjoyable"
3. PROOF — "I understand what it is and why it is different"
4. OWNERSHIP — "I can picture us using it"
5. HESITATION — answer the strongest objection
6. TIMING — real reason to act now
7. ACTION — one clear CTA

Do not stack urgency before trust and relevance are established.
