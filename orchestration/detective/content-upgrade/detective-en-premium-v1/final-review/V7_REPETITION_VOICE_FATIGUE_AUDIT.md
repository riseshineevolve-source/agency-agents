## Repeated structural language

The spatial cases necessarily repeat some vocabulary:
- record / placement / room / companion / witness / area.
This is partly mechanics, not style.

But the *function* repeats:
"solve placement -> identify same-room contact -> they may clarify what happened."

Cases most exposed to this sameness:
02,04,06,07,10,12,13,15,17,19,20,22,23,25.

Case29 uses the same spatial engine but has a stronger final coordinate function.

## Reader-fatigue risk zones

### Cases 02-07
Low risk.
New mechanics, low difficulty, fast novelty.

### Cases 10-15
Medium risk.
The book alternates spatial and nonspatial, but Case13 spikes mechanically and Case15 then drops.

### Cases 17-25
Highest repetition risk.
Many "contact" spatial cases occur while the meta mystery is intentionally slow-burning.
Story consequences need to be visible enough that each map feels like a different investigation, not another roster exercise.

### Cases 26-30
Low repetition risk.
The book finally reuses earlier artifacts, which makes old work feel meaningful.

- no therapy clichés;

- avoid characters naming character development too often.
