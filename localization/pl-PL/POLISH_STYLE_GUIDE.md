# Polish Style Guide for RSE

## Target standard
Write contemporary, idiomatic Polish that feels authored in Poland, not translated from English. Prefer clarity, rhythm and emotional precision over structural loyalty to the source.

## Default register
- natural standard Polish
- warm and direct
- conversational where appropriate
- no bureaucratic stiffness
- no generic coaching voice
- no forced youth slang
- no excessive diminutives

## Rhythm
Polish usually benefits from restructuring English sentence order. Split overloaded English noun stacks. Merge choppy fragments when Polish cadence needs it. Keep short punchy fragments only when they sound intentional in Polish.

## Translationese to remove
Avoid literal carry-over of:
- English noun chains
- unnecessary possessives
- repeated personal pronouns
- repeated gerunds rendered mechanically
- 'make X happen' structures
- 'designed to...' repeated across marketing copy
- 'whether you are...' boilerplate
- English capitalization patterns
- excessive title case

## Humor and wordplay
Do not translate jokes mechanically. Identify the comic mechanism: surprise, contrast, exaggeration, double meaning, rhythm, callback, cultural recognition or character voice. Recreate that mechanism in Polish. The Polish joke may use different imagery.

A successful transcreation may substantially change wording while preserving:
- target emotion
- narrative function
- relevant facts
- character identity
- age suitability

## Children 7-10
- intelligent, lively, concrete
- playful without baby talk
- sentences easy to follow aloud
- avoid adult coaching vocabulary
- prefer images, action and character voice over abstraction
- slang only when genuinely natural and durable

### Gender-neutral direct address
Polish direct address to a child must not create clumsy gender workarounds.

Final child-facing prose must not use:
- slash forms such as `gotowy/gotowa`
- parenthetical endings such as `zrobiłeś(-aś)` or `Kapitan(a)`
- duplicated masculine/feminine sentences unless the product explicitly requires separate variants

Prefer restructuring the sentence so it is naturally gender-neutral. Use imperatives, second-person present tense, noun-free direct address, infinitive structures, or a different sentence rhythm.

Examples:
- avoid: `Gotowy/gotowa na misję?`
- prefer: `Zaczynamy misję?`
- avoid: `Jesteś Kapitanem/Kapitanką tej wyprawy.`
- prefer: `To Ty tu dowodzisz.`

Gender neutrality must never make the Polish stiff or bureaucratic.

## Teens
- never imitate TikTok slang merely to sound young
- use concise, self-aware language
- allow gaming language when the product intentionally uses it
- avoid parent/teacher voice unless narratively required

## Adults / parents
- empathetic without therapy-speak overload
- practical rather than preachy
- use humor to lower pressure, not trivialize emotion

## Brand voice
Rise.Shine.Evolve Polish copy should feel:
- warm
- smart
- energetic
- lightly irreverent
- useful
- emotionally aware
- unforced

Preferred effect: a small knowing smile, not a comedy routine.

## Recurring labels and in-world terminology
Recurring labels are product language, not disposable prose. Examples include mission names, callout headings, character catchphrases, buttons, recurring exercise labels and coined in-world concepts.

Before locking a recurring Polish term, verify:
- meaning and narrative function
- natural Polish
- audience fit
- visual length on the intended surface
- compatibility with previous accepted occurrences
- whether the phrase will still sound natural after frequent repetition

Once a recurring term is accepted, add it to the project glossary and treat it as locked regression terminology. Do not casually introduce synonyms later.

For recurring branded character headings, validate against the character glossary before inventing a Polish label. A clever one-off translation is not enough if the heading recurs throughout a book or app.

## Coined terms and metaphors
RSE products may deliberately invent labels, metaphors and playful mini-systems. In Polish they must look intentionally invented, not accidentally mistranslated.

A coined Polish term should have:
- a clear function in context
- readable morphology
- an obvious playful or narrative reason to exist
- consistent typography if typography helps signal that it is an in-world term
- enough durability to recur without becoming awkward

If the literal English coinage sounds accidental in Polish, recreate the concept instead of preserving the wording.

## Emotional-support claims
Do not strengthen an emotional, wellbeing or educational promise during transcreation.

If the source says an activity `can help`, `may remind`, or `can make it easier`, the Polish version must not silently become a guaranteed outcome such as `uspokoi Cię`, `poprawi Ci nastrój` or `sprawi, że poczujesz się dobrze`.

Prefer calibrated phrasing such as:
- `może pomóc`
- `łatwiej wtedy...`
- `może przypomnieć`
- `spróbuj i zobacz, co działa u Ciebie`

Preserve uncertainty and modality as carefully as facts.

## Surface length and designed copy
For buttons, labels, book callouts, exercise headings, cards and other designed surfaces, naturalness alone is not enough.

Before acceptance, check:
- fit in the intended container
- wrapping risk
- hierarchy and scan speed
- whether the Polish expansion changes the visual rhythm
- whether a shorter native phrase can preserve the same function

Do not shorten by turning good Polish into a calque. If the source label cannot fit naturally, localize the function rather than compressing word-for-word.

## Formatting and typography
Use Polish quotation marks „…”. Use an en dash for ranges and a proper dash according to Polish typographic context. Preserve product/brand names unless a deliberate localized title has been approved. Use Polish date, number and punctuation conventions.

## QA question
Before accepting a sentence, ask: `Czy dobry polski autor naprawdę napisałby to właśnie tak, gdyby nie widział wersji angielskiej?`
If not, rewrite.

For child-facing and designed copy, also ask:
- `Czy to zdanie działa bez podawania płci dziecka?`
- `Czy ten termin wygląda na celowo wymyślony, a nie źle przetłumaczony?`
- `Czy obietnica nie jest mocniejsza niż w źródle?`
- `Czy ten label naprawdę zmieści się i będzie działał po setnym użyciu?`
