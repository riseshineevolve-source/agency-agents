# E16 — THE ROOF THAT FOLDS INSIDE OUT
Date: 2026-10-12; Tome 1; portrait 8.5 x 11; status: CONCEPT SELECTED, image candidate requested, NOT GOLD.
Authority: Studio bootstrap + 2026-10-11 two-volume execution plan. Design produced editorially this run; NO claimed external agent jury.

## Three substantially different complete creative concepts
### A. The House on Its Own Horns
Worm's-eye high oblique along mansion roof entirely camera-rolled -70 degrees LEFT. A large anatomical stag walks across one diagonal outside slate-plane (correct hooves, non-human posture). One impossible continuous strip of roof starts as *outer shingles* and folds 180° at an enormous three-dimensional hinge to become the *floor inside an illuminated room* revealed through the roof itself; NO literal hole or mundane cutaway box. At fold, alternating thick black and broad WHITE Op Art bands turn through a volumetric corner like curved metal/ribbon. The stag's antlers visually align with two interior stair banisters, but they are clearly organic anatomy and not extra limbs. Secondary reveal: the supposedly open dormer window is actually facing INTO this interior floor, despite being on outer roof. No moon/skeleton/mirror floor. **SELECTED**, perspective and optical paradox dominate.

### B. The Falling Balcony
Full -70-degree camera roll: stag clings to underside of tiled roof as though gravity changed; hanging rain gutters form a mechanically impossible cantilevered balcony. Giant roof stripes bend into stag rib contours. Rejected: too close to E03 inverted bedroom and E08 dual gravity; antler anatomy risks merging with railings.

### C. The Chimney Without an Outside
Extreme macro through stag antlers, giant inverted chimney acts like a corridor, negative-space roof forms a second deer profile in clouds (geometric not real sky). Rejected: roof/interior relationship weaker, likely another animal portrait with striped architecture rather than a true impossible viewpoint. Keep as Volume 2 idea only if reworked.

## Editorial ranking, qualitative
A: strongest narrative contradiction, distinctive exterior/interior switch and camera-rolled structure. Risks clutter, need print-safe white zones and distinguish antlers/rails.
B: strong movement but repeats gravity scenes and likely physics ambiguity.
C: high first-glance beauty but lack of extreme whole-world tilt.
No numerical "agent jury" or final-art PASS before visual inspection.
