# Audience / family-ear diagnostic — rejected V9

All 34 scenes reviewed against their actual case setup and earlier wording. This is an editorial assessment for ages 8–12, not a child test or a psychological assessment. No replacement candidate was written here.

## Overall direction

V9 often gives a good joke, explains it, then returns the family to correct detective behavior. That turns affection into supervision and slows the book. The problem is not simply word count: a longer exchange can work when the next speaker wants something and changes the comic situation. A short instruction can still flatten a scene.

Keep the family’s unevenness. Mimi loses her phone and can be caught. Luli is sharp and can be caught reading instead of eating. Dilo wants to win but is also useful. Alio deserves both pointed teasing and an ally who joins his invention. Nini names things and understands people; she should not become everyone’s tiny teacher. Bibi has pride, taste and fallible memory; she need not regulate each sentence.

Best belonging signals are shared food, a defended drawing, clearing table space, someone remembering your habits, and being invited to do a useful part. They do not need to be followed by an explanation of inclusion. Maintain the unusually quiet care in 21 and collective patience in 29. The entire book need not sound equally funny at all times.

Vocabulary is not an automatic veto. A young reader can infer a harder phrase when a physical joke makes it clear. Avoid a chain of adult-office metaphors (promotion, appointment, qualifications, policy, identities) that makes following the joke harder than the case. No forced slang, no compulsory moral, no requirement that every tease apologize for itself.
