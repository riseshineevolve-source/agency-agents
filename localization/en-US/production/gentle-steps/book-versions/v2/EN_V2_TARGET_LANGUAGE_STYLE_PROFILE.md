# Gentle Steps EN V2 — Target Language & Style Profile

## Edition target

Primary market: United States  
Secondary compatibility: United Kingdom / international English-speaking families  
Audience: families with children roughly 8–12; adults participate equally  
Experience: approximately 10 minutes each evening

## Core voice

Modern premium family-book English.

It should feel:
- warm but not syrupy;
- witty but not gag-heavy;
- specific rather than inspirational;
- emotionally intelligent without therapy language;
- written for real households, not an idealized holiday commercial;
- equally readable by an adult and an 8–12-year-old;
- slightly dry/self-aware in the humor;
- comfortable with imperfection.

The authorial stance is:
**We know December is a lot. We are not giving you another project. Here is one small useful pause, one genuinely fun thing to do together, and one short conversation worth having.**

## Preferred labels

Provisional reader-facing labels:
- **PAUSE** = function of Polish ZWOLNIJ
- **PLAY** = function of Polish GRAMY
- **BETWEEN US** = function of Polish MIĘDZY NAMI
- **TRY IT TOMORROW** = practical transfer cue

These remain an owner-facing brand choice until content approval, but all working text should use them consistently.

## Language

US-first, internationally readable English.

Prefer:
- school
- work
- practice
- backpack
- water bottle
- parking
- grocery run
- holiday errands
- wrapping paper / tape
- packages
- charger
- dishwasher
- laundry
- couch / sofa only when context allows neutral wording
- holiday break / winter break where relevant

Avoid unnecessary dialect traps:
- do not make "parking lot", "car park", "vacation", "term", "grade", "football/soccer" central unless needed;
- choose neutral wording when the joke does not depend on dialect.

## Humor

Calibration:
- short;
- attached to the exact scene;
- never more sentimental than the main copy;
- characters must sound different.

Dilo:
- systems;
- rules;
- QA;
- timing;
- tech;
- mock-official language;
- sports logic when natural.

Luli:
- dry precision;
- practical deadpan;
- elegant understatement.

Alio:
- energetic literalness;
- cheerful chaos;
- fast brain / movement observations.

Nini:
- sideways mischief;
- confident small-person logic;
- slightly cheeky, never babyish.

Mimi:
- domestic reality;
- body/common-sense observations;
- warm practical humor.

Happy Makers:
- warm collective closing;
- no motivational-poster voice.

## Anti-cringe blacklist

Do not use as default framing:
- mindful / mindfulness
- presence
- gentle invitation
- sacred pause
- heart glow
- inner light
- shared energy
- gratitude circle
- family synergy
- calm flowing through us
- magic as filler
- sparkle as filler
- connection-building as explicit explanation
- "regulate emotions"
- "deepen bonds"

These concepts may exist functionally, but the reader-facing copy should normally show them through ordinary family life.

## Emotional-pressure rules

Never force:
- touch;
- eye contact;
- closing eyes;
- praise;
- apology;
- forgiveness;
- disclosure;
- singing;
- performance;
- physical closeness.

Where relevant:
- provide PASS;
- provide open-eyes route;
- provide no-touch route;
- provide two-person route;
- do not shame opting out.

## Quality bar

A new English line only replaces the Polish-derived function if it is:
1. naturally English;
2. culturally believable;
3. at least as clear;
4. at least as funny/warm where humor exists;
5. equally or more usable;
6. no more pressuring;
7. compatible with the 10-minute promise.

If not:
KEEP THE FUNCTION and rewrite again.
