## Product truth

The English edition is NOT a translation of the Polish edition sentence by sentence.

The polished Polish R4 is the current authority for:
- the 24-day architecture;
- the three daily functions;
- activity/game mechanics;
- progression;
- safety / consent / agency;
- two-person and group-size handling;
- the practical next-day transfer cue;
- the stronger humor philosophy;
- the final-week arc;
- the Day 24 callback finale;
- back-matter function;
- overall quality bar.

The old English book remains a legacy source for:
- historical title/branding;
- Happy Makers names and legacy bios;
- English phrases or concepts that are genuinely strong;
- legacy front-matter ideas worth retaining.

It is not allowed to reintroduce weaker:
- mindfulness/coaching clichés;
- forced gratitude;
- forced touch/eye contact;
- weak/repetitive games;
- vague instructions;
- old Human Knot;
- old singing-only finale;
- repetitive late-book reflection;
- generic inspirational language.

## Core target

Create a premium native English edition primarily for US families and naturally readable in the UK.

The target family:
- has school, work, sports/after-school activities, errands and holiday logistics overlapping;
- often reaches the evening with unfinished tasks still running;
- experiences gift/shopping pressure and financial awareness;
- uses screens/messages/notifications heavily;
- may be traveling, hosting or negotiating extended-family plans;
- wants Christmas to feel meaningful without adding another obligation;
- wants fun that children 8-12 will actually join and adults will not find childish.

## Writing rule

Do not localize by swapping Polish nouns for American nouns.

Localize the **life pattern**.

Examples:
- "something for school tomorrow" may become forgotten spirit-day item, school project, lunchbox/water bottle, permission slip, band/sports gear, teacher note, etc. only when it fits naturally;
- "traffic" may be school pickup, practice, grocery run, parking lot, holiday errands;
- "shopping pressure" may include online orders, delivery boxes, last-minute gift lists, wrapping tape, missing batteries;
- UK-compatible alternatives should avoid overly US-exclusive jargon unless the context clearly benefits from it.

The book should sound like one excellent native English author, not a Polish book wearing US vocabulary.
