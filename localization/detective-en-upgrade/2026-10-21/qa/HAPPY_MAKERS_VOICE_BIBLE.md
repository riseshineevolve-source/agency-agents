## How authentic comms work

1. **A particular person wants something.** Alio wants to contribute; Dilo wants to win; Nini wants to care for or name the odd thing. A generic joke begins at a pun. A Happy Makers scene begins at a want.
2. **Someone who knows them reacts.** Luli recognizes Dilo's pre-boast; Mimi knows Alio has already been staring. This implies shared history without a backstory paragraph.
3. **The first speaker adjusts and keeps agency.** Alio revises his theory; Dilo corrects his watch; Nini accepts that Inspector Glitter needs relevance. Never make adjustment equal defeat.
4. **A second speaker adds or redirects, not simply repeats.** Each turn changes what the next person can say. Five individually witty remarks do not constitute a conversation.
5. **The final beat lands in relationship or action.** They keep working, share a glance, protect a source, allow a point, or wait for the reader. It need not end with another evidence maxim.

A scene generally needs two or three active speakers, not all six. Four to six short turns is often enough. Do not expand every page to an equal quota. Let a sharp joke remain sharp, let a reveal remain quiet, and give a big puzzle room to breathe.

Keep puzzle instructions in exact literal English. Character licence belongs in file narrative, comms, story bridges and optional margin reactions. Comms must not introduce a new fact that changes the set of solutions; a joke about soup is not evidence of a dragon's location.

## Affectionate teasing boundaries

Tease a choice, a boast, an overgrown hat, or an object's imaginary status. Do not tease a child's intelligence, body, distress, reading speed, need for help, or unwillingness to be touched. The person being teased gets an answer or is visibly included in the next action. Rotate who gets the last word. Mimi and Bibi are available targets; Alio must not bear all the comic cost.

A useful diagnostic is the **next action**: after the line, does someone help, listen, share, return an object, give room, or wait? If nothing in the scene reveals that they like one another, add one small action or change the response. Do not add an explanatory sentence that they all love each other.

GENTLE STEPS p14 permits a shoulder tap only if both people are comfortable; p20 makes shoulder contact optional; p36 says a navigator does not touch the other person; p37 allows a pass; pp42–43 exclude private/embarrassing questions; p47 rejects compulsory apologies and compulsory forgiveness; p48 makes holding hands optional. These are useful direct boundaries for newly written warmth. They do not require a safety paragraph in Detective. Choose unobtrusive, natural behaviour: an offered high-five, sitting nearby, a file held steady, a snack saved, or simply time.

At current p98, after Dilo tears the paper, **“Nobody laughs”** is essential. Add immediate reassurance or shared repair before the methodological instructions, not a comedy roast. The later line about nobody tearing anything else can feel like surveillance if not balanced by help. His consequence is that the team must reconstruct the note; his personhood stays secure.
