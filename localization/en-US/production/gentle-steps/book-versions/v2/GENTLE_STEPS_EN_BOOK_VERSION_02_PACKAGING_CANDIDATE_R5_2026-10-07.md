# BEFORE DAY 1

December can be wonderful and completely unreasonable at the same time.

School is still happening. Work is still happening. Practices, concerts, errands, packages, grocery runs, gift lists, laundry, messages, wrapping paper, missing chargers, and things somebody suddenly needs for tomorrow are also still happening.

This book is not here to become one more thing on that list.

Each day gives your family three short pieces:

## PAUSE

About a minute to take one layer of pressure off.

No perfect breathing. No special mood. No requirement to feel calm.

Every PAUSE also includes **TRY IT TOMORROW**, so the useful part can leave the page and come with you into an ordinary school day, commute, practice, argument, waiting room, or overloaded evening.

## PLAY

A quick family game designed for real living rooms and real energy levels.

No shopping list. No craft supplies. No elaborate setup.

The goal is not to win the holidays. The goal is to laugh, cooperate, notice each other, and have one good thing happen in the middle of an ordinary December night.

## BETWEEN US

One short conversation.

Not a family meeting. Not a lesson. Not a forced heart-to-heart.

One question that gives everyone a chance to say something real, funny, useful, or worth remembering.

If a day takes ten minutes, great.

If it takes six because somebody has homework, somebody is hungry, and somebody has reached their lifetime limit for family activities, that still counts.

The Happy Makers will be here too, usually with a comment, a rule, or a suspiciously specific observation about family life.

Ready?

Day 1 starts by taking something off the list.

## DAY 1

Before we begin, one important thing: this book is not supposed to become another item on your December to-do list.

So today starts by removing something, not adding something.

### PAUSE: ONE MINUTE WITHOUT "I HAVE TO"

December is extremely good at adding the words "I have to."

I have to remember. Buy. Bring. Send. Wrap. Finish. Get there. And apparently all of it should happen today.

Sit together somewhere comfortable. Rest your hands on your lap or let them fall loosely by your sides.

For one minute, breathe normally.

Each time you breathe out, think only: **not right now**.

The list is not disappearing. For sixty seconds, you simply do not have to work on any part of it.

If another task pops into your head, leave it there for this one minute. You will still know about it when the minute is over.

**TRY IT TOMORROW:** If five different "I have to" thoughts show up at once before school, work, practice, or bedtime, choose one thing to do now and tell the rest: not right now.

Luli: If we check off nothing for one minute, December will probably survive. Testing now.

### PLAY: THE INVISIBLE BALL

**START:** Stand in a circle. The person whose name comes last alphabetically begins.

**ROUND 1:** The starter pretends to hold an invisible ball.

Every time the ball is thrown, one thing about it changes. It might weigh a ton, float like a balloon, be tiny as a pea, or huge as an armchair.

Do not announce what changed. Show it with your body.

The person catching it reacts as if that ball is real, changes one feature, and throws it to someone who has not caught it yet this round.

The round ends when the ball has visited everyone.

**ROUND 2:** The ball goes back to normal size.

This time, when you throw it, tell the person catching it one specific good thing you like about them or noticed lately.

Not "you're awesome." Something real.

The catcher just says, "Thanks," then passes the ball to someone who has not heard a Round 2 sentence yet.

The final catcher sends the ball back to the starter, so everyone receives one specific good thing.

**TWO PEOPLE:** Toss the ball back and forth. Change its properties in Round 1. In Round 2, each of you tells the other one specific good thing.

Nini: No full wind-up. We're delivering a compliment, not testing reflexes.

### BETWEEN US: WHAT DO I LIKE ABOUT DECEMBER, AND WHAT AM I DONE WITH?

Everyone finishes two short sentences:

**"In December, I like..."**

**"In December, I am tired of..."**

You can like the lights, the music, the smell of something baking, holiday movies, or being home under a blanket.

You can be tired of traffic, crowds, noise, tests, shopping, wrapping, or constantly trying to remember what still needs to happen.

Nobody has to defend an answer.

Luli: You can love Christmas and still be completely done with the question "Where is the tape?" at 10:40 p.m. Both can be true.

## DAY 2

### PAUSE: FOUR IN, SIX OUT

Some December mornings begin with:

"Where is my homework?"

"Was I supposed to bring something today?"

"Where is my other glove?"

and somebody's breakfast quietly getting colder in the background.

If today was one of those mornings, sit comfortably for a minute.

Breathe in while counting gently to four.

Breathe out while counting to six.

Do not pull in a huge breath. If this rhythm does not feel comfortable, go back to your normal breathing.

Keep going for about a minute.

**TRY IT TOMORROW:** Before a test, performance, game, difficult conversation, or anything that makes you feel rushed, try a few four-in, six-out breaths if that rhythm feels good to you.

Mimi: The missing glove cannot get much farther away in one minute. Search temporarily suspended.

### PLAY: ORCHESTRA WITH NO REHEARSAL

**START:** Stand or sit in a circle. The person with the next birthday begins by making one short sound, such as "boom," "ding," "pop," or "whoop."

**PLAY:** Going clockwise, each person repeats all the sounds that came before, in order, then adds one new sound.

When everyone has added one, stop.

Now perform the whole sound sequence together from beginning to end.

**FINALE:** Do it two more times: once a little quieter, then once a little faster.

If it falls apart, do not restart. Find the sequence again wherever you are.

**TWO PEOPLE:** Take turns adding sounds until you have a six-sound sequence. Then perform it together.

Dilo: If the third version still sounds strange, it is no longer an error. It is the arrangement.

### BETWEEN US: WHAT DO WE LIKE ABOUT OUR FAMILY?

Take turns finishing:

**"One thing I like about our family is..."**

It can be important or completely ordinary.

Maybe someone always remembers who hates tomatoes.

Maybe somebody starts laughing at dinner and everyone else follows.

Maybe mornings go wrong in six different ways and you still somehow get out the door.

One real thing from each person is enough.

Luli: One true sentence is plenty. The family anthem can wait.

## DAY 3

### PAUSE: LEAVE IT BY THE DOOR

All day long, everyone is carrying something.

At school you remember things, listen, hurry, keep track, and try not to forget what comes next.

Work is not especially different.

Then everyone meets at home carrying backpacks, bags, hunger, and the entire day with them.

Before moving on, stand or sit for a moment.

Feel both feet on the floor.

Let your shoulders drop.

Take three ordinary breaths. If it feels comfortable, let the breath out last a little longer than the breath in.

Think of one thing from school, work, or today that you do not need to keep carrying in your head for the next ten minutes.

**TRY IT TOMORROW:** After school, an argument, or a hard moment, use three breaths and your feet on the floor to stop dragging the whole thing behind you for a few minutes.

Alio: Backpack stays by the door. The test can stay there too. At least for ten minutes.

### PLAY: FREEZE FRAME

**START:** Stand or sit in a circle. The youngest person gives the first prompt.

**PLAY:** When the prompt is called, everyone, including the caller, immediately makes a pose and freezes for three seconds.

Prompts can be normal or ridiculous:

"first snow"

"the bus is late"

"someone just remembered something they need tomorrow"

"the gift you definitely were not supposed to find"

After three seconds, relax.

The next person clockwise gives the next prompt.

Play five or six rounds.

**FINALE:** Make the last prompt a whole-family scene, such as: **"leaving the house three minutes late."**

**TWO PEOPLE:** One person gives the prompt. Both freeze. Then switch.

Alio: The best poses are the ones nobody can explain three seconds later.

### BETWEEN US: WHAT HAPPENED TODAY THAT WE DO NOT KNOW YET?

Everyone tells one small thing from today that the rest of the family has not heard yet.

It does not have to be important.

Maybe somebody said something ridiculous.

Maybe something funny happened on the way somewhere.

Maybe the thing that was supposed to fail actually worked.

Maybe you spent half the day looking for something that was in your backpack the entire time.

One short scene is enough.

Luli: Finally, a day update without asking, "So... how was school?"

## DAY 4

### PAUSE: ONE LIGHT ONLY

December enjoys flashing several things at once.

Lights. Screens. Notifications. And the three things you just remembered you forgot.

For a moment, choose one small light source, such as a lamp, a light on the tree, or a candle an adult has placed safely.

Watch the light move, flicker, or reflect on things around it.

Breathe normally.

You do not have to analyze anything.

For this minute, your eyes get one job.

**TRY IT TOMORROW:** When screens, notifications, and several tasks are pulling your attention in different directions, look at one still point for a few breaths and give your eyes only one thing to do.

Luli: One thing at a time. Somebody should probably tell December.

### PLAY: SAME LINE, DIFFERENT STORY

**START:** The person with the longest hair chooses one ordinary sentence, for example:

"Has anyone seen my charger?"

"Is there one cookie left?"

"Are we leaving now?"

**PLAY:** The first person says the sentence normally.

Then each person repeats exactly the same words but with a different intention.

Try:

1. breaking news
2. a huge secret
3. suspicious interrogation
4. sports commentary
5. someone who has just made an earth-shattering discovery

After each version, everyone else guesses the intention.

Do not repeat an intention within the same round.

**FINALE:** Choose a new sentence and play without using the list. The speaker picks the intention secretly. Everyone else guesses.

**TWO PEOPLE:** Take turns saying the sentence. The listener gets one guess.

Mimi: One "Where is the charger?" can carry a surprising amount of plot.

### BETWEEN US: WHAT WENT DIFFERENTLY THAN PLANNED TODAY?

Everyone chooses one thing from today that did not go the way they expected.

It may have gone better, worse, or simply stranger.

The bus showed up immediately.

A test was easier than expected.

Dinner changed at the last minute.

Somebody said something nobody saw coming.

No lesson required. Just tell the short version of what happened.

Dilo: So the plan was more of a suggestion. Like most plans after 4 p.m.

## DAY 5

### PAUSE: THREE LAYERS OF SOUND

Some afternoons everyone is talking, something is beeping, something else is playing, somebody is looking for something, and the laundry is still running in the background.

For one minute, do not add another sound.

If you want to, close your eyes. If you would rather keep them open, rest your gaze on one still point.

First, listen for what is closest to you.

Then notice what you can hear farther away: behind a door, outside, or in another room.

Finally, find the quietest sound you can still hear in the background.

Do not guess what it is.

Just listen.

**TRY IT TOMORROW:** When the bus, school hallway, house, or car gets too loud, stop adding your own noise for a moment and listen for three layers: close, farther away, and quietest.

Dilo: Quiet. My brain just closed three tabs. Only seven left.

### PLAY: 12 WITHOUT A MISTAKE

**START:** Sit in a circle.

**LEVEL 1:** The tallest person starts. Count to 12 in order, going clockwise. Each person says one number.

**LEVEL 2:** Now there is no turn order.

Anyone may say the next number, but you cannot decide in advance who will speak.

If two people say the number at the same time, or somebody says the wrong number, go back to one.

**LEVEL 3:** Same rules, except 3, 6, 9, and 12 are claps instead of spoken numbers.

**FINALE:** If you reach 12, try again from 12 back to 1 with no turn order.

**TWO PEOPLE:** In Level 1, alternate. In Levels 2 and 3, after every number either person may say the next one, including the same person twice in a row. If you speak at the same time or make a mistake, go back to one.

Dilo: Replay review: two voices at once. Call overturned. Back to one.

### BETWEEN US: WHAT SOUND TELLS YOU, "I'M HOME"?

Everyone names one sound that immediately feels like home.

It might be someone's laugh, a key in the door, a dog running across the floor, the kettle, the washing machine finishing, or music coming from another room.

One sound each.

Alio: The refrigerator door opening. You do not even have to look to know somebody is checking the situation.

## DAY 6

### PAUSE: NO MATCHING PACE REQUIRED

After school, work, traffic, and after-school activities, everyone gets home at a different speed.

Somebody is hungry.

Somebody wants to tell a story immediately.

Somebody would prefer not to answer a question for five full minutes.

Sit comfortably with your hands resting on your lap.

For four breaths, touch your thumb to each fingertip in order: index, middle, ring, little finger.

Move at your own pace.

You do not have to count, deliberately slow down, or finish at the same time.

Then stay with your normal breathing for another moment.

**TRY IT TOMORROW:** When an answer is already coming out because everyone is rushing or something has annoyed you, touch your thumb to all four fingers at your own pace before you speak.

Alio: Nobody is saying "faster." Good. I support this activity.

### PLAY: EVERYONE AT ONCE

**START:** Stand side by side in one row. The youngest person begins.

**ROUND 1:** Keep your feet in place.

The leader calls: "slightly forward," "slightly back," "left," or "right."

Everyone leans the same way and comes back to center.

Do eight moves.

**ROUND 2:** The next person by age becomes leader.

Now use tiny steps: forward, back, left, or right.

Try to complete 12 shared steps without anyone going the wrong way. If someone does, reset the step count to zero and keep going.

**FINALE:** Together, choose a four-move sequence.

Practice once with a leader.

Then perform it three times with no spoken directions.

If you lose the sequence, just start it again.

Keep every movement small and safe.

**TWO PEOPLE:** Play exactly the same way. In the finale, try the sequence at the same pace without counting out loud.

Luli: If left and right came in different colors, this would be suspiciously easy.

### BETWEEN US: WHAT ACTUALLY HELPS TO HEAR?

Everyone names one sentence or a few words they like hearing from someone in the family when they have had enough.

Sometimes it is:

"I've got it."

"I saved you some."

"You do not have to answer right now."

"Want a minute?"

Choose something that actually works in your home.

Luli: "I've got it" has roughly the same December value as finding a parking space right away.

## DAY 7

### PAUSE: LET THE FLOOR DO ITS JOB

Evenings have a strange habit of arriving after everyone is already tired, while dishes, backpacks, things for tomorrow, and at least one missing item are still waiting.

Somehow, this is also when the dishwasher becomes invisible.

Sit or lie down near each other, wherever you are comfortable.

Let your hands, legs, and back fully rest against the floor, couch, or whatever is supporting you.

Notice where your body makes contact.

For one minute, let the surface underneath you do most of the holding.

Breathe normally.

You do not need to tighten, fix, or hold anything up.

**TRY IT TOMORROW:** After a long day, practice, carrying a backpack, or being on your feet, lean back or lie down for a moment and let the surface underneath you take some of the weight.

Mimi: The floor wants absolutely nothing from you. One of its best qualities.

### PLAY: CHAIN REACTION

**START:** Stand one behind another, all facing the same direction. The person at the back starts.

**PLAY:** The starter invents a simple two-part gesture, such as one clap plus a thumbs-up.

They let the person in front know it is time to turn around.

They may lightly tap a shoulder if both people are comfortable with that, or simply say the person's name.

Show the gesture once.

That person turns to the next person and passes on exactly what they remember.

The gesture travels all the way to the front.

The first person in line performs the version that arrived.

Only then does the starter show the original.

**FINALE:** Play three rounds. A different person starts each time.

In the last round, the gesture may have three parts.

**TWO PEOPLE:** One person shows the gesture once. The other copies it. Switch roles, then try a three-part gesture.

Nini: If "clap and thumbs-up" arrives as "salute and sneeze," nobody fixes it. That is officially the message we received.

### BETWEEN US: WHO DID SOMETHING THAT ALMOST NOBODY NOTICED?

In December, a surprising number of things look as if they happen by themselves.

They do not.

Everyone chooses one person in the family who recently did something helpful or quietly took care of something that would have been easy to miss.

Say exactly what you noticed.

If that person is with you, finish with a normal "Thanks."

Nini: Interesting. Mugs do not return themselves to the kitchen, and backpacks do not pack themselves for tomorrow. We have suspects.

## DAY 8

### PAUSE: YOUR HEART IS ALREADY DOING ITS JOB

After a day of remembering a hundred things, your heart has been quietly doing its job without needing any extra instructions.

Sit comfortably.

If you want to, rest a hand on your chest. If not, leave your hands on your lap.

For a moment, notice your heartbeat or simply the small movement of your breathing.

If you cannot feel anything clearly, that is fine too.

No counting.

No comparing.

No checking whether anyone else feels the same thing.

Stay here for about a minute.

Nothing needs to be planned or finished during this minute.

**TRY IT TOMORROW:** If you are waiting for a result, your turn, or an answer and your body starts speeding up, notice a few normal breaths. Put a hand on your chest if you want to; leave your hands on your lap if you do not.

Nini: One thing today that did not need a reminder. Impressive.

### PLAY: THE STORY NOBODY PLANNED

**START:** Sit or stand in a circle. The oldest person begins with exactly three words, for example:

"Reindeer walked into..."

**PLAY:** The next person adds exactly three words.

Then the next person adds three more.

Nobody rewinds or fixes the story, even when it turns toward complete nonsense.

Go around twice.

If someone gets stuck, they may use the three words:

**"and then suddenly"**

**FINALE:** Choose the strangest scene from your story.

Count "three, two, one," then everyone acts it out for a few seconds with no assigned roles.

**TWO PEOPLE:** Add three words at a time until each person has had five turns. Then act out your chosen scene together.

Alio: If the plot still makes sense after two rounds, somebody is controlling this suspiciously well.

### BETWEEN US: WHAT WAS HARDER TODAY THAN IT LOOKED?

Everyone names one thing that took more effort today than other people could see.

Maybe it was a test.

A conversation.

Waiting.

Concentrating.

Being kind while tired.

Handling something at work.

Or simply making it to the end of the day.

Do not turn it into a speech.

One sentence is enough.

If you do not want to talk about it today, you may say **pass**.

Mimi: Not every hard thing makes a lot of noise.

## DAY 9

### PAUSE: THREE, TWO, ONE

If one mistake, conversation, or worry is still replaying in your head, do not analyze it for the next minute.

Give your brain a simpler job.

Sit comfortably and look around.

Find **three different colors**.

Then listen for **two sounds**.

Finally, notice **one place where your body is touching the chair, couch, or floor**.

Do not say the answers out loud.

Take one normal breath and return to the evening.

**TRY IT TOMORROW:** When the same mistake, conversation, or worry starts looping again, do a quick three-two-one: three colors, two sounds, one contact point.

Nini: Three colors. Two sounds. One floor. Finally, a list we can actually finish.

### PLAY: PLAN B: 60 SECONDS

**START:** Sit or stand together.

The person who got home last today begins.

If everyone was home all day, the youngest person starts.

That person reads the first situation.

You do not need a timer.

"60 seconds" means: make a quick plan without spending half the evening discussing it.

**RULE:** Go around in order.

Each person adds exactly one concrete step to the shared plan.

You may not delete the previous person's idea.

You may improve it or add the next step.

Before the plan is finished, everyone needs one useful role.

Nobody gets the role "do everything."

**ROUND 1:** You need to leave in five minutes.

One glove is missing.

The water bottle is empty.

The keys are "definitely somewhere."

The dog is sitting by the door with the leash and making a very clear point.

Build a plan, one step per person.

At the end, answer only:

**"Would this plan actually get us out the door?"**

If not, you get one quick repair. Each person may change or add one detail.

**ROUND 2:** Dinner is almost ready.

The laundry just finished.

Someone cannot find a charger.

And somebody has just remembered one thing that needs to be ready for school or work tomorrow.

Build one shared plan again.

**FINALE:** Invent your own harmless family classic: a moment when four things suddenly need to happen at once.

You get one round to turn it into Plan B.

**TWO PEOPLE:** Play the same way. Each person gets two turns in each round, and you still may not erase the other person's idea.

Dilo: Plan B does not have to be elegant. It just has to work before somebody discovers Plan C.

### BETWEEN US: WHAT SAVED THE DAY A LITTLE?

Take turns naming one small thing that happened between you, or at home, that made today even slightly easier.

Maybe somebody made tea.

Lent you a charger.

Remembered something important.

Made you laugh at exactly the right moment.

Or simply sat down next to you.

One example each.

Luli: If someone saved you the last cookie, we are not calling that a small thing. That is an event.

## DAY 10

### PAUSE: SIDE BY SIDE, NO PLAN

Sometimes everyone is finally home, but nobody has actually stopped.

Someone is finishing homework.

Someone is answering a message.

Someone is thinking about dinner.

Someone is hoping nobody asks them for anything for the next five minutes.

Sit near each other.

If everyone is comfortable with contact, you may let your shoulders touch lightly.

For a few breaths, do not try to match breathing or fix anyone's mood.

Everyone keeps their own pace.

Do not decide what happens next.

Just sit here for a moment.

**TRY IT TOMORROW:** If someone comes home with no interest in talking, or clearly needs a little quiet, try sitting nearby for a moment without fixing the situation.

Alio: Nobody is coordinating anything and somehow it still works. Please do not ruin this by asking questions.

### PLAY: THE FAMILY MACHINE

**START:** The person whose name comes first alphabetically begins.

**PLAY:** The starter invents one simple repeating movement and one short sound.

Keep both going.

The next person joins with a different movement and sound.

One at a time, everyone adds their own part until the entire machine is running.

Hold the shared rhythm for about 15 seconds.

**FINALE:** Without choosing a leader, try to:

1. speed up a little
2. slow down
3. stop the whole machine at the same moment

You may not choose a secret signal in advance.

Watch one another and catch the change from the group's movement.

If it falls apart, restart the machine and try again.

**TWO PEOPLE:** Each person has one movement and sound. The finale is the same: speed up, slow down, and stop together with no planned signal.

Dilo: The machine does not need a purpose. It does need to stop at roughly the same time. Standards.

### BETWEEN US: THE LINE WE KEEP SAYING

Everyone names one sentence that regularly appears in your home.

Maybe:

"Where are the keys?"

"Who has the charger?"

"I'm coming!"

"Please do not leave that there."

Or something that would mean nothing to another family but turns up constantly in yours.

Choose one that sounds most like your house.

It does not have to be wise.

The more real it is, the better.

Luli: "Where are the keys?" has one clear advantage over a family motto. It actually gets used.

## DAY 11

### PAUSE: FOREHEAD AND JAW OFF DUTY

If today included several rounds of:

"Hurry up."

"It's my turn."

"Where is it?"

or

"We were supposed to remember that,"

there is a decent chance your face is still holding part of the conversation.

Sit near each other.

Close your eyes if you want to. If you would rather keep them open, lower your gaze.

For a few normal breaths, check whether your forehead is scrunched or your jaw is tight.

If it is, let it loosen a little.

No goal.

No need to check whether everyone is doing the same thing.

Finish with one more easy breath and let your face stay off duty.

**TRY IT TOMORROW:** Before answering while angry or saying "hurry up" one more time, check your jaw and forehead and loosen them for one breath out.

Nini: Jaw is off duty. Even if somebody just remembered something they need tomorrow.

### PLAY: WHAT HAPPENED HERE?

**START:** Sit or stand in a circle. The person wearing the most colorful socks invents one harmless household mystery, for example:

"Why is there a spoon in a shoe?"

"How did a pillow get into the hallway?"

"Why is the remote under the table?"

**PLAY:** Going clockwise, everyone gets about ten seconds to offer one explanation.

It can be absurd, but for one second it should sound as if it might actually have happened.

Nobody chooses a winner.

Just hear all the versions.

Then the person on the starter's left invents a new mystery.

Play three mystery rounds total, with a different mystery-maker each time.

**FINALE:** Choose one earlier explanation and build on it together, with each person adding one new detail.

**TWO PEOPLE:** One person invents the mystery. The other explains it. Switch roles. Do three mysteries each.

Luli: The best part is when the ridiculous explanation starts sounding disturbingly possible.

### BETWEEN US: WHAT ARE WE ACTUALLY GOOD AT?

Take turns finishing:

**"Our family is genuinely good at..."**

It can be impressive or completely ordinary.

Rescuing dinner at the last minute.

Packing for a trip.

Making each other laugh.

Finding missing things.

Doing absolutely nothing together.

Everyone may give a different answer.

Alio: If the answer is "finding things nobody else can see," I accept the result without review.

## DAY 12

### PAUSE: ONE LOOK

You can spend half a day in the same house, pass each other in the kitchen and hallway, and barely look at one another.

Sit together and take one normal breath.

For a few seconds, look at someone near you.

You do not have to look directly into their eyes.

Think of one specific thing you like or value about that person.

Do not say it.

Do not make a meaningful movie face.

One look is enough.

**TRY IT TOMORROW:** When everyone is passing each other in a rush and you realize you are talking through a doorway or over a screen, stop for one second, look at the person, then say what you were going to say.

Mimi: Relax. This is not a competition for Most Meaningful Eye Contact.

### PLAY: FIVE WORDS. TWO LIES.

**START:** Sit or stand in a circle. The person with the longest first name secretly chooses a recognizable person, animal, object, or character from a movie, book, game, or show.

**PLAY:** Describe your choice using exactly five individual words:

1. three words are true clues
2. two words are completely made up

Example for Santa:

**Sleigh. Beard. Reindeer. Sushi. Skateboard.**

Everyone first guesses which two words are lies.

Then they guess who or what the clues describe.

The next chooser is the person to the right.

Continue until everyone who wants a turn has chosen once.

**FINALE:** The last chooser may pick something harder, but at least one other person should have a fair chance of knowing it.

## A LETTER FROM THE HAPPY MAKERS

If you are here, you made it through 24 December days together.

Maybe you did every day in order.

Maybe you skipped some.

Maybe there were ten-minute evenings.

Maybe there were four-minute evenings because someone was hungry, someone had a test, someone was stuck in traffic, somebody had practice, or one person had absolutely no interest in a family activity.

That counts too.

This book was never supposed to turn December into a perfect month.

It was supposed to help you catch a few moments that are easy to miss while everything else is happening.

Maybe someone said something you did not know before.

Maybe your family created a joke nobody else would understand.

Maybe one game rescued a bad evening.

Maybe somebody heard one specific "thank you" or one specific good thing at exactly the right time.

Maybe the to-do list genuinely waited for one minute.

Do not turn these 24 days into a new obligation now.

Keep only what felt like yours and actually worked.

On an ordinary Tuesday, you can throw the Invisible Ball again.

After a hard day, you can sit beside someone without asking questions.

When the house is too loud, you can remove one sound instead of trying to silence everyone.

When four things happen at once, you already know how to build a Plan B.

When something between you goes wrong, you can come back, apologize, and try again.

A good home does not have to be quiet all the time.

A good December does not have to be finished perfectly.

The ordinary days on the way to Christmas count too.

Thank you for letting us into your December.

**Happy Makers**
