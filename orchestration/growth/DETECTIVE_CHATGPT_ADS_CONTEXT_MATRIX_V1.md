## WHAT the product is

Happy Makers Detective Academy: The Mystery of Room Zero is a screen-free, pencil-ready mystery/puzzle book for ages 8–12 built around 30 interconnected mystery missions and one book-long mystery. The reader joins the squad as the missing detective. The experience includes varied evidence-led puzzles, a 3-level Hint Vault and reasoning-based solutions.

## WHO it fits

Primary buyer:
- parent/caregiver shopping for an 8–12-year-old who enjoys mysteries, logic, codes, maps or detective play;
- gift buyer seeking an activity gift that feels like an experience rather than a generic worksheet or puzzle collection.

Primary user:
- child aged about 8–12 who wants agency, challenge and discovery rather than school-like exercises.

## WHEN / conversation situations

High-fit situations:
1. “I need a screen-free activity for an 8–12-year-old who likes mysteries.”
2. “I need a puzzle/mystery gift that feels substantial and not babyish.”
3. “My child likes detective stories but also enjoys logic and codes.”
4. “I want an offline activity that can keep a child engaged across multiple sessions.”
5. “I want a puzzle book where getting stuck does not ruin the experience.”

## Conversation-context variants to test later

### Context A — Screen-free mystery
Situation: parent wants a compelling offline activity.
Message direction: “A screen-free mystery adventure they actually get to enter: 30 interconnected missions, a blank detective badge, and one larger mystery running through the whole book.”

### Context B — Gift experience
Situation: gift buyer wants something more immersive than a standard puzzle book.
Message direction: “More than a puzzle book. A case they get to enter: evidence boards, maps, codes, Hint Vault support and a book-long mystery.”

### Context C — Child likes logic + story
Situation: buyer wants both narrative and reasoning.
Message direction: “Each case can be solved, but earlier evidence can matter again later. The story keeps moving while the child follows clues, tests deductions and builds the larger mystery.”

### Context D — Gets frustrated when stuck
Situation: buyer wants challenge with graceful support.
Message direction: “The 3-level Hint Vault gives progressively stronger nudges before the full solution, so getting stuck stays part of detective work instead of ending the experience.”
