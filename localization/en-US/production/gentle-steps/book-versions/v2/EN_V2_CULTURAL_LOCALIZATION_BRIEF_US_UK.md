# Gentle Steps EN V2 — US-Primary / UK-Compatible Cultural Localization Brief

Date: 2026-10-06

## Evidence used

### United States
- University of Michigan C.S. Mott National Poll on Children's Health: holiday stress commonly comes from extra shopping/tasks, finances, family gatherings and special meals; some parents report their stress harms children's enjoyment; help from family is one of the stress reducers.
- National Retail Federation 2025: 91% of US adults planned to celebrate winter holidays; average planned seasonal spend was about $890; 42% planned to start shopping before November, yet 60% still expected to finish in December; avoiding last-minute stress is a major reason for early shopping.
- APA 2024: family gatherings can also carry relational/political tension for many adults.

### United Kingdom
- YouGov 2025: one-third of Britons are typically worried about Christmas's impact on finances; concern is especially high among 35-49-year-olds, the age group most likely to have young children; women report more Christmas stress and much more of the gift-shopping load.
- Ipsos 2024/2025: Advent calendars remain a major mainstream tradition; family gift expectations and affordability are meaningful pressure points.
- Action for Children 2024: many UK children/young people are aware of parental money worries around Christmas.

## Editorial implications

The emotional truth is similar to Poland:
- too much to do;
- not enough time;
- shopping/gift pressure;
- school/work still running until late in December;
- food/hosting/travel logistics;
- children carrying their own school/social expectations;
- family members arriving home at different energy levels;
- screens/messages/notifications competing for attention;
- parents trying to manufacture a "perfect" Christmas while ordinary life continues.

The localization should therefore preserve the Polish R4's core promise:
**do not add another holiday task; create a small pocket of useful relief, laughter and family connection.**

## US-primary life texture

Use naturally, not mechanically:
- school pickup / drop-off;
- sports practice, dance, music, clubs;
- school concerts, spirit days, projects, permission slips, teacher gifts only when relevant;
- lunchboxes / water bottles / backpacks;
- holiday parties;
- packages on the porch;
- delivery notifications;
- gift lists;
- wrapping tape;
- batteries;
- grocery runs;
- crowded parking lots;
- traffic between school, practice and errands;
- cookie trays / hot cocoa / holiday movies;
- visiting relatives / hosting / travel days;
- Christmas lights, stockings, tree, Santa traditions;
- leftovers, laundry, dishwasher, missing chargers;
- the "everyone is home but the day is not over" feeling.

Avoid over-Americanization:
- no brand names;
- no sports-team assumptions;
- no mandatory church/religious framing;
- no claim that every family celebrates the same way;
- keep "Christmas" because that is the product, but write inclusively around family structures and levels of religiosity.

## UK compatibility

Prefer wording that also reads naturally in Britain:
- "school" rather than US-only grade terminology unless necessary;
- "practice" is acceptable but avoid stacking US-exclusive terms;
- "parking lot" can be avoided when "parking" or "the car park" would create a dialect split;
- "vacation" should usually be "break" or "time off";
- "cookies" is natural in US; when not essential use "something from the kitchen" / "last treat" to avoid constant dialect marking;
- Santa / Father Christmas should not be made a recurring identity issue.

## Humor calibration

US/UK English should be:
- drier than children's-TV banter;
- quick;
- specific;
- slightly self-aware;
- rooted in the exact family situation.

Character tendencies:
- Dilo: systems, rules, timing, mock-QA, sports/tech precision.
- Luli: precise dry practicality and elegant deadpan.
- Alio: literal, energetic, optimistic chaos.
- Nini: sideways mischief and bold small-person logic.
- Mimi: grounded household/body reality and warm domestic comedy.
- Happy Makers: warm collective close, never poster-like.

Avoid:
- "magical" in every paragraph;
- "sparkle" as filler;
- "heart" metaphors as default;
- therapy language;
- "mindful" as a repeated label;
- over-explaining the lesson;
- fake Gen-Z slang;
- forced Britishisms or Americanisms.
