## H — The House That Remembers

**Narrative spine:** A mysterious toothless key leads a visitor to a house holding an interrupted welcome, not a lost prisoner; accepting the invitation restores the missing minute. The physically recurring evidence is a three-notch negative space, one empty seat and a shadow that arrives early.

**Act 1 (scenes 1–4):** Invitation With No Arrival.  
**Act 2 (scenes 5–8):** Rooms That Remember Backwards.  
**Act 3 (scenes 9–12):** The House Looks Like a Trap.  
**Act 4 (scenes 13–16):** The Protective Truth.  
**Act 5 (scenes 17–20):** A Welcome Completed.

## C — The Midnight Carnival Below the City

**Act 2 (scenes 5–8):** Rules of the Silent Midway.  
**Act 3 (scenes 9–12):** The Ringmaster's Apparent Crime.  
**Act 4 (scenes 13–16):** Why the Last Beat Vanished.

## Collection distinction and QA gates

- **House**: intimate domestic objects and foldable architecture. Emotional payoff is the completed invitation; cat only an occasional witness.
- **Carnival**: kinetics and performance, with tension between spectator and participant. Emotional payoff is accepting an imperfect shared beat; no villainous clown imagery.
- **Museum**: sculptural stillness, future-bearing glass and absent bodies. Emotional payoff is releasing rather than hoarding an unrealized night; crow only an occasional symbolic exhibit.
- **Cross-collection prohibition:** reuse no identical optical mechanism + hero + camera triplet. In particular, not every scene may be a hallway, mirror hall, central circular portal, raven or black cat.
- **Scene production rule:** before each hero artwork create 3–5 genuinely distinct concept routes; Visual Storyteller ranks, Brand Guardian vetoes generic routes, selected composition is generated, Reality Checker audits geometry, Print Finish-Gate verifies real-scale print, strongest source protected as BEST without destructive overwrites.
