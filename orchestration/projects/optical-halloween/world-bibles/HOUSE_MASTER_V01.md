## Elevator pitch

A visitor accepts a toothless key from a silent townhouse and discovers that its locked rooms are holding one unfinished minute of midnight. Domestic objects seem to rehearse her imprisonment, but their reversed gestures preserve a host's interrupted promise of welcome. She must read the house accurately, repair the storm-damaged route to its front door, and accept the single place already laid for her before the house can let time—and its threshold—open again.

The visual identity is **hospitality caught inside folded architecture**: heavy black domestic structures, large white receiving spaces, precise hinges, displaced shadows, and intimate household objects made monumental by viewpoint. Halloween comes from the midnight threshold, a house behaving with memory, deserted preparations, an unexplained interruption, and occasional glimpses of a watchful adult black cat. Dread turns into recognition without becoming cute.

## Protagonist, stakes, and cast

**Mara** is an adult visitor whose curiosity is stronger than her first alarm. Her consistent silhouette is a knee-length straight coat, plain trousers, practical low boots, short swept-back hair, and an asymmetrical notch in her left coat cuff. Her proportions remain human and adult; five fingers where visible, believable wrists, stable shoulder width, and feet that bear weight. Avoid glamorous costume styling, a witch outfit, exaggerated eyes, or an ornamental face. She discovers the invitation rather than receiving a written assignment. The key rises into the doorstep's small bell tray when she approaches.

**The house** has agency but no face, teeth, tentacles, or speaking walls. Its single stored memory is a host attempting to bring a cup to an empty dinner place and open the front door during a midnight storm. A lifted roof slate jammed the weather linkage at the instant the host raised the table's serving leaf. The house preserved that unfinished movement by folding a minute into its connected domestic hinges. The absent host is never depicted as a ghost or a corpse. No person is imprisoned in a reflection.

**The adult black cat** is a witness in H-02, H-08, H-17, and H-20 only. Use a mature, ordinary short-haired cat with a clear ribcage, pelvis, jointed hind legs, plausible ears, and a tail emerging from the sacrum. Render the coat as crisp black masses with necessary white anatomical separations, not fuzzy fur or pasted patterns. It neither performs repairs nor provides cartoon clues.

**Stakes:** until the movement is completed, the return route remains folded and the clock cannot advance through its last minute. Mara initially believes the house intends to keep her. She can force no safe direct exit through the folded route, but she can choose to stop probing or undertake the repair. Her eventual acceptance is voluntary: the empty chair was prepared for a visitor, and she occupies it briefly herself. She is not replacing the host, surrendering her identity, or feeding an unseen entity.

## Physical and visual rules

1. **One governing optical mechanism per image.** Ordinary perspective and incidental specular details may support it; they must not become competing illusions. Candidate alternatives replace the selected mechanism rather than accumulate on top of it.
2. **Reflection-dependent key rule.** The smooth, toothless blade stays smooth in reality and in every reflection. A receiving slit opens in the actual latch only while Mara observes that latch and the key through a real plane mirror. Looking directly closes it. This is an explicit fantasy condition on a real aperture, not permission to invent teeth, a different reflected object, or a floating portal. H-01 and H-16 are its two deliberate uses.
3. **One active impossible seam.** The stored minute travels along a visibly connected hinge and service-linkage route; only one local seam is active in a scene. It moves from piano fallboard to table serving leaf to the under-stair landing as Mara advances the release. Geometry away from that seam remains Euclidean, solid, and weight-bearing. A seam may change adjacency, never arbitrarily duplicate an object, resize Mara, or turn a reflection into a new room.
4. **Mirror accountability.** Identify every reflective surface and its plane. Reflections must show existing objects at the corresponding location, scale, and orientation; a left hand remains a left hand in mirrored coordinates. A cut mirror may interrupt a view, not manufacture absent anatomy. Keep fragments coplanar in H-11. Floor reflections lie beneath their corresponding objects on the same horizontal plane.
5. **Shadow accountability.** Each shadow scene has one specified light source, real occluders, and a receiving surface. A silhouette may suggest a second meaning through designed object geometry. No independent shadow actor, unexplained second light, or detached cat shadow.
6. **Refraction accountability.** H-06 uses lateral inversion through a cylindrical water jug with the object sufficiently far behind the cylinder; only the transmitted portion changes. H-15 uses displacement through a triangular glass prism; it does not run the clock or reflect the whole room. The art must visibly distinguish the real object from its optically shifted portion.
7. **No writing inside art.** The promise is an unlettered gesture: an offered cup, an open receiving hand, and an empty place. It appears reversed in actual reflections at H-02, H-11, and H-19. The clock has broad unnumbered hour indices. Its stopped long hand approaches the crown from the 59-minute position; H-15 makes that offset legible at large scale, and H-20 closes it. No written invitation, clock numerals, labels, letters, signatures, or borders.
8. **Gallery image first.** Compose for 8.5 × 11 inches, portrait. Aim flexibly for 40–55% solid near-black coverage, represented as crisp ink-black rather than gray shading. Give the hero a strong thumbnail silhouette and then reveal a second reading through real geometry. Density follows planes, folds, joints, and material edges; it never spreads as uniform swirly wallpaper.
9. **Colorable space is structural.** Each selected direction names broad white forms. Prefer connected interiors with usable closed contours and a few spacious open fields. A provisional design floor is about 0.2 inch across in principal white channels, with several hero zones closer to 0.5 inch or larger at intended size; these are concept targets requiring later image and print checks, not measured results. Essential contours should not rely on hairlines, tiny gaps, hatch shading, or microscopic white islands.
10. **Negative lock.** No pumpkins, ghost sheets, graveyards, stock Halloween vectors, generic mandalas, gray gradients, fuzzy fur, tiny ornamental noise, decorative text, or interior frames. Do not turn roof tiles into a carnival tent, the pantry into a museum display, or the cat into the page hero by default.

## Five-act causal plot

**Act I, H-01–H-04: entry and paradox.** Mara learns the key's reflected-view condition, enters a foyer whose floor shows a reversed domestic promise, follows its cuff contour to a glove cabinet, and uses the glove to raise a damaged umbrella. Its actual shadow identifies the roof profile that caused the interruption.

**Act II, H-05–H-08: backwards domestic memories and rule.** The lifted slate exposes a jammed linkage but cannot be reached securely from the current folded landing. The linked vent leads to a cold stove and the real invitation cup. Optical reversal at the stove teaches Mara to distinguish reversed presentation from intention. The cup opens a piano hinge whose single active seam leads to the prepared guest bedroom; its architectural echoes show the same offer repeatedly stopping short of the dining place.

**Act III, H-09–H-12: escalation and false clues.** The bedroom opens onto a stair position where an anamorphic latch silhouette looks like a barricade. A low chessboard view reinforces the mistaken prison reading, then its overlap exposes access to the attic. A broken wardrobe mirror fragments the actual offering relief and makes it look grasping. Mara follows its real service rod down to the dining room, where the table leaf folds the return route around one empty chair. The apparent trap now has a specific physical cause.

**Act IV, H-13–H-16: intention revealed and choice.** A pantry grip and cup cradle reconstruct an open receiving hand, proving that the apparent grasp was incomplete hospitality. The bell bracket shows how a raised serving leaf pulls the entrance latch shut while the storm linkage is jammed. The large clock view distinguishes the missing minute from an endless time loop. Mara chooses to complete the welcome and uses the original key, watched through a correctly placed mirror, to release the table hinge.

**Act V, H-17–H-20: healing and welcome.** The release unfolds the under-stair landing, restoring a safe roof route. Mara reseats the storm slate, which frees the entrance rod. She returns the cup to the prepared place and accepts the invitation by sitting there. The last minute completes; the closing vestibule echoes the first threshold composition, with the door open, shadows changed by its new angle, and the cat physically free to pass.
