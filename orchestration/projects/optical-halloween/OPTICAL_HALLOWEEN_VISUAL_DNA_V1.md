# OPTICAL HALLOWEEN — gallery-art visual DNA v1
Date: 2026-10-08
Status: proposed technical language, board and source image QA required before creative freeze
Relationship: inherits *quality architecture* from Optical Animals; does not copy its animal compositions or its repeated vortex/tunnel motif.

## The actual differentiator
A picture must remain strikingly artistic even when the Halloween identifier is removed, while the distinctive optical event must be inseparable from the Halloween narrative when it is present. The visual puzzle is **in the structure of the work**, not an artificial hidden-object task.
Every hero has (a) one primary subject or architectural gesture readable at thumbnail size, (b) one optical mechanism with a comprehensible physical rule or one intentionally bounded paradox, (c) clean genuinely usable white surfaces, and (d) a second visual meaning discovered at closer viewing.

## Absolute collection constraints
- **Medium:** premium ink-black and untouched white, not charcoal airbrush, blurred grayscale painting, fuzzy outlines, stock vector or zentangle.
- **Frame:** planned 8.5×11 portrait art area. Never add text, labels, chapter numbers, signatures, ornamental borders, KDP templates, or worksheet panels to the artwork. No book assembly in this lane.
- **Black / white ratio:** target approximately 40–55% near-solid black in typical hero compositions, yet reject forced black fill if it damages scene hierarchy. Amount of real colorable white surface on the HERO and main scene matters as much as global coverage.
- **Linework:** crisp dimensional ink with visible contour hierarchy: heavy black anchors, midweight form edges, few hairline structural accents. Fine lines must survive at real print size; reject microscopic white fragments and texture noise. Do not certify a proposed image before a full-size print-scale inspection.
- **White zones:** broad islands with closed or clearly usable boundaries; deliberately reserve a few calming zones amid dense optical fields. A dense optical scene may be more difficult than Optical Animals, but never become uncolorable.
- **Subject:** recognizable mature anatomy and credible joints in animals or humans. Avoid toy/chibi faces, stuffed animal proportions, fuzzy realistic fur, disconnected hands, floating heads, physically incoherent reflected paws or limbs, and unintended extra silhouettes.
- **Halo/portal embargo:** repeating the same centered spiral, cathedral arch, checker corridor, round moon disk, or mirrored hall is disallowed. Strong iconic shapes may appear once or twice with a distinctive purpose, never as background wallpaper.
- **No generic Halloween:** no standard table pumpkins, cute sheet ghosts, stock broom witch, stock cemetery, grinning clowns, decorative spiderweb filling void, franchise imitation or gratuitous gore.

## Rules of optical perception
1. **Specular reflection:** give the real reflecting plane a defined position, camera, surface normal and a visible physical source. The image in it must correspond to the source position/pose/scale, except for one clearly intentional fantasy deviation.
2. **Refraction:** use one lens/prism to deflect a few lines with orderly continuity; a warped lens must preserve recognizable entry/exit of a pattern, not create mystery extra objects.
3. **Forced perspective / anamorphosis:** define the intended privileged viewpoint; off-axis fragments cohere into the second shape only from that viewing direction.
4. **Impossible architecture:** use a single identifiable discontinuity (stairs fold to wall, opening reads inside/outside, velvet becomes roof, exhibit plane opens into future). Other gravity, occlusion and contour logic should be rigorously consistent.
5. **Cast shadow as story:** determine a believable source direction and projection on each plane; a shadow can mean a future/absence, but it cannot accidentally contradict the scene's base geometry.
6. **Tessellation and repetition:** attach rhythm to a physical cause—fold, leg, chandelier, stage machinery, mask edge, stair step or glass facet. Deliberately move between dense/calm/dense, not same density everywhere.
7. **Negative-space reveal:** at thumbnail read the hero, at closer read notice the second image formed only by gaps and selective black regions. Do not render the second image as clip-art pasted over the first.
8. **Reflective magic must be bounded:** if a glass exhibit shows tomorrow, only the glass is prophetic. No uncontrolled second heads or duplicated characters elsewhere.

## Camera and choreography discipline
Across each set of 20 vary: 3–5 extreme close-ups, 3–5 low worm's-eye, 3–5 high overhead/bird-eye, 3–5 editorial obliques/corner perspectives, plus long wides, sectional cuts or truly unusual inverted views. These counts are *working diversity targets*, not a rigid production quota. Never repeat the same composition grammar in more than two consecutive hero artworks. Gallery consistency comes from tonal balance, optical rigor and narrative causality rather than repeated layouts.

## Art production handoff and version history
- Each scene: propose 3–5 **truly different** shots/mechanisms before selecting one. Visual Storyteller ranks; Brand Guardian vetoes ordinary design; Image Prompt Engineer engineers actual geometry; Reality Checker audits objects, reflections, narrative clue; Print Finish-Gate reviewer examines output **at real 8.5×11 scale** before considering PASS.
- Name stable concept IDs like H-01, C-01, M-01. Save generated art as `OH_H01_<slug>_v01.png`, `..._v02.png` etc. This is *image candidate naming*, not page numbering/assembly.
- Never overwrite previous BEST; preserve original prompt intent, date, dimensions, critique and source provenance. Owner review only for genuinely strong A/B or a novel visual-language gate, not every prompt repair.
- No false approval labels: `CONCEPT_ONLY`, `GENERATED_UNREVIEWED`, `QA_HOLD`, `BEST_CANDIDATE`, and only with image evidence `PRINT_SCALE_VISUAL_PASS`. A good thumbnail alone is never PRINT_PASS.
- Actual gallery illustrations remain separate from Optical Animals. Book/factory tasks are explicitly deferred.

## Narrative lock across all worlds
The three series explore one abstract theme—what a missing moment means—without forced cross-world character/IP crossovers. **House preserves memory of a welcome**, **Carnival suspends a musical response**, **Museum protects a future not yet lived**. Each has distinct internal cause, stakes, reveal and final visual transformation. Image #20 must alter a motif planted in #01–04, not simply display a generic sunset / exit sign.
