# Polish Book Version 02 — Owner Style + Research Brief

Status: ACTIVE CREATIVE BRIEF

## Owner target

This edition should feel written from inside a real Polish December.

It must recognize:
- rushed school mornings;
- breakfast, missing PE clothes, forgotten school items, unmatched socks, lost notebooks;
- traffic and being late for extracurricular activities;
- hunger and overstimulation after school/work;
- fights over the bathroom, music and shared space;
- the moment everyone comes home and the pressure of school/work finally drops;
- evening exhaustion, clutter, chores and the pile of things still waiting;
- the feeling that everyone is tired and nobody wants one more task.

The book should not promise a calm December.
It should offer a few practical minutes that help a family move through a real one.

## Daily function

Each day still contains exactly:
1. a short breath / quiet / grounding moment;
2. a game;
3. a short family conversation / sharing moment.

## Quiet / breath

The reset should:
- name a real situation first;
- use a concrete body, breath, sensory or attention action;
- never promise therapeutic results;
- avoid mindfulness jargon;
- feel useful in the actual moment;
- sometimes help the family leave school/work pressure at the door;
- sometimes reduce the sense of "I have to do everything now";
- sometimes create a brief pause before reacting.

Research note:
A 2025 systematic review of diaphragmatic/slow breathing studies in ages 6–18 found generally promising stress-related outcomes, but the evidence base is heterogeneous, so the book must keep claims modest and practical rather than medical.

## Games

Games should primarily create:
- laughter;
- cooperation;
- coordination;
- shared challenge;
- trust;
- support;
- adults and children playing on equal footing.

Research note:
Studies in children 7–12 found dynamic group games associated with greater group-energy impressions and higher trust than conversation-only conditions. Research on structured cooperation in ages 8–12 also supports clear interdependence and mutual coordination.

Design rule:
Every game should answer:
- What makes this fun in the first 20 seconds?
- What do we have to do together?
- What is the funny failure state?
- Can an adult lose or look ridiculous too?
- Is there zero or almost zero setup?

## Between us

The conversation layer should rotate across:
- current feelings;
- ordinary daily experiences;
- appreciation;
- gratitude;
- family memories;
- what home gives us;
- what helps when the day is hard;
- wishes and small plans;
- apology;
- family identity;
- funny discoveries about one another.

Research note:
SW Research reported in December 2025 that 26% of surveyed parents spent under 3 minutes daily on serious conversation with a child and another 30% under 6 minutes. A separate Polish holiday-conversation survey found about half of respondents wanted less superficial, more connecting family conversations.

Design rule:
A child must be able to answer in one sentence.
A deeper answer is welcome but never required.

## December truth

Polish 2025 research repeatedly described pre-Christmas rush and stress as a real concern. 45% in one SW Research survey named avoiding pre-holiday rush/stress as a reason for shopping earlier.

Therefore:
- do not write "slow December";
- write "real December, plus one small moment that belongs to us";
- recognize chaos without making the book itself chaotic.

## Happy Makers humor

Humor must be:
- brief;
- specific;
- character-consistent;
- based on the exact family situation or game;
- self-aware;
- never generic cheerleading;
- never fake teen slang;
- never a joke at the expense of vulnerability.

The best comments should feel like the sentence a family repeats later.
