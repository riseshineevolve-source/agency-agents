## Page 11

MAP CASES
READ THIS ONCE

Some cases use an Academy floor grid to reconstruct where people were standing. Use these rules unless a case gives a more specific instruction.
1 COORDINATES
Letters label columns from left to right. Numbers label rows from top to bottom.
D3 = column D, row 3. Use the labels printed beside the map you are solving.
2 PLACE THE WITNESSES
Use only printed witness statements, the map key and visible map features.
1. Place every witness exactly once, on a usable square.
2. Each witness must use a different row and a different column.
3. Start with a certain position. Cross its row and column off the options for everyone else, then check the clues again.
4. “Exactly one other person” means two people in that named room or area in total. Use this clue while placing people, not just after you finish.
5. A room companion is a lead for the next interview, not automatically guilty.
6. Keep every final position on the completed map. Do not erase it after recording the verdict.
3 CLUE WORDS
BESIDE: one square up, down, left or right, in the same room or area. Diagonals and squares across a wall do not count.
DIAGONALLY NEXT TO: exactly one row and one column away; the two squares touch at a corner.
NORTH / SOUTH / EAST / WEST: up / down / right / left. North-west means above and left; the two distances need not match. The same applies to the other paired directions.
A row-distance clue fixes only the row difference; a column-distance clue fixes only the column difference, unless the clue also specifies the other direction.
GRID STEPS: add the row difference and the column difference. Count grid distance, not a walking route around walls.
OUTER WALL: the outside edge of the whole grid.
4 READ EACH CASE'S MAP KEY
A printed open circle means an object square is usable. A printed black square means it is blocked. Follow the marker when an object has one.
Otherwise, use clear floor and only the objects permitted by that case's key. Other object squares are blocked.
Use named room or area boundaries when checking who shares a space. Case 26 gives its own room-and-zone key for the later map task.
