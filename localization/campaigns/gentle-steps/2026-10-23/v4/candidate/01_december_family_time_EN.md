# How to Find Family Time When December Is Already Full

By bedtime, you've reminded everyone about shoes, homework, dinner and whatever they need for school tomorrow. You've talked all day. Somehow, you haven't had time to hear what made anyone laugh.

December doesn't cancel work, school or laundry. It adds concerts, presents and the urgent discovery that tomorrow is Christmas jumper day. The jumper is, naturally, in the wash.

Coming up with a lovely family activity can feel like one more thing to remember. Having an idea ready makes that part a little easier.

## Start while the evening is still unfinished

Someone's clearing a plate. Someone else has claimed the comfortable end of the sofa. This can be the moment to ask, "Anyone fancy doing something together?" You can start with whoever wants to join. The rest of the evening can stay as it is.

**Before Christmas Slips By**, from Rise.Shine.Evolve. and The Happy Makers, is a family Advent calendar with 24 ready-to-use days. Each has a short pause, a game and a question to talk about, taking around ten minutes altogether.

Here are small glimpses from **three different days**.

### LET’S TAKE A MOMENT

**Empty Hands.** Put the phone, mug or tea towel somewhere safe. Rest your hands on your knees. For a minute, there's nothing to carry, clean or put away. When did your hands last have absolutely nothing to do?

### LET’S DO THIS TOGETHER

In the **Family Machine**, everyone adds a repeating movement and sound of their own. Soon the living room sounds like a factory nobody knows how to switch off. Part of the challenge is stopping all at once, without agreeing on a signal. Someone will probably still be going.

### LET’S TALK

**“Who’s done something helpful lately that almost nobody noticed?”**

Perhaps someone helped a younger sibling find a lost glove, or remembered the thing everyone else forgot. You might want to say, "Thanks for doing that. It helped." Or you might want to hear what someone else noticed. There's no compulsory round of speeches.

## Leave room for the evening you actually have

Five minutes may be enough tonight. Two people can use a two-person version of a game. If everyone needs a quiet evening on their own, skip a day; tomorrow won't come with a double session. The small **TRY IT TOMORROW** idea gives you a way to use a brief pause during an ordinary day, too.

There are no extra supplies to shop for or activities to set up beforehand. You get to sit down and join in, rather than spend the whole time getting things ready for everyone else.

The laundry will still be there afterwards. For a few minutes, you might have something else to talk about.
