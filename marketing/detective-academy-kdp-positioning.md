## Strategic position

Do **not** position this as "Murdoku for kids" or as a generic educational puzzle workbook.

The product category may be mystery/logic puzzle books, but HMDA's owned territory is:

> **30 interconnected mystery missions. One secret hiding across the whole book. You're the missing detective.**

Core distinction:

- A normal puzzle book is a collection of puzzles.
- HMDA is a **book-long detective adventure** in which the reader becomes a member of the squad.
- Solved cases later become evidence.
- The physical book itself functions as part of the mystery.

Primary positioning idea:

> **THE BOOK ITSELF IS THE EVIDENCE.**

Secondary hook:

> **Every case can be solved. Not every case is finished.**

Do not spoil the locked `CHECK THE OLD MAP` message or Room Zero reveal in marketing.

## Reader promise

For the child:

> **Become the missing detective.**

For the parent:

> **A screen-free mystery adventure they'll actually want to solve.**

For gifting:

> **More than a puzzle book. A case they get to enter.**

The educational benefit is supportive proof, not the lead promise. Lead with adventure, mystery, agency and discovery. Then support with logical thinking, observation, persistence and evidence-based reasoning.

## Owned product pillars

### 1. Reader-as-detective psychological ownership

The opening black envelope, blank Academy badge and line:

> **ONE DETECTIVE IS STILL MISSING.**

are not decorative copy. They are a signature product mechanic.

The reader writes their own detective name and joins the Happy Makers squad.

Marketing should make this visible early:

> A black envelope arrives. Inside is a Detective Academy badge. The name line is blank. **It belongs to you.**

Avoid presenting the reader as a passive puzzle solver.

### 2. One escalating Book 1 mystery

Book 1 has 30 missions, but it must be sold as:

> **30 MISSIONS + ONE BOOK-LONG MYSTERY**

not as "only 30 puzzles."

The recurring zero, Rule Zero, evidence walls, old Academy connection, reopened cases and final Room Zero payoff create the series-like narrative spine.

A solved case may become relevant again later.

Safe teaser:

> **Keep your old case files. Some evidence becomes important only after you think the case is closed.**

Never reveal the actual 14-case meta message in public copy.

### 3. Second-use clue / reopen-the-case signature mechanic

Standard puzzle loop:
`solve -> answer -> next`

HMDA loop:
`solve -> close case -> later discover that earlier evidence matters again`

This should be treated as a signature mechanic across:
- back cover,
- Amazon description,
- A+ / listing graphics if available,
- launch reels,
- free Case Zero funnel,
- future Book 2 design.

### 4. Hint Vault without shame

HMDA should not punish a child for getting stuck.

Locked product idea:

> **STUCK IS PART OF DETECTIVE WORK.**

The 3-level Hint Vault should be actively marketed as a feature.

Desired progression:
- nudge 1: what to notice,
- nudge 2: where to focus,
- nudge 3: stronger deduction help,
- full solution only when wanted.

Solutions should explain **why**, not merely reveal the final answer.

The experience should leave the child feeling more capable after a difficult case.

### 5. Premium detective-object experience

The interior should feel like an object from the Detective Academy archive, not a school worksheet.

Visual / editorial signals:
- case dossiers,
- mission ranks,
- Witness Boards,
- evidence cards,
- case-file stamps,
- agent field notes,
- old photographs,
- maps,
- badge / certificate,
- Room Zero,
- clear evidence-wall interludes.

Do not over-gamify with fake currencies, points economies or screen-like clutter.

### 6. Happy Makers / RSE universe

Happy Makers are the recurring squad.

Case-specific people may use a deterministic branded adventure alias layer.

Owner-approved naming direction: **B — branded / academy / adventure**.

Style examples only:
Nova, Echo, Blaze, Clover, Scout, Pixel, Dash, Sage, Juno, Atlas, Rook, Skye, Moxie.

Rules:

### 7. Screen-free without sounding anti-tech

The parent benefit is real-world concentration and satisfying pencil-on-paper play.

Use phrases such as:

- pencil-ready case files,
- offline detective challenge,
- a gift that becomes an experience.

Do not moralize about screens or shame parents/children.

## Kids-engagement design rules

Before KDP freeze, every material design choice should be tested against:

1. **Can a child read it immediately at print size?**
2. **Does it make the child feel like an agent, not a student?**
3. **Is there a visible next action?**
4. **Does the page reward noticing rather than guessing?**
5. **Is challenge progressive rather than suddenly punitive?**
6. **Does the child have a graceful route when stuck?**
7. **Does the world feel coherent enough to remember?**
8. **Does any visual decoration accidentally reveal puzzle logic?**

Print readability wins over decorative density.

White map backgrounds, bold room/zone names, larger coordinates, readable legends and generous pencil space are locked presentation principles.
