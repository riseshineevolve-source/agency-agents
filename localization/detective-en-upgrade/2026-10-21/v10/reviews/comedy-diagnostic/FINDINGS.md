# Comedy and native English diagnostic

**Verdict: FIX.** This reviews the rejected V9 baseline, not an unpublished V10 candidate. All 34 units and 183 existing turns were read against the case introductions, task neighbours, original and V7/V8 references. No candidate text was edited.

V9 often contains a sound comic nucleus but explains it before or after it lands, adds another metaphor, and ends with a task reminder. V7 frequently has sharper punches but sometimes leaves conversational intent unclear. Rebuilding responsiveness is more useful than padding or globally shortening.

## Across the book

- Give each scene one intelligible comic premise and a small change of position: boast, challenge, defence, reversal, allowance. Not every scene needs that exact structure.
- Let short answer turns stay short when the surrounding context carries them. Expand only where motivation, response or physical action is missing. No word target or scene-length uniformity.
- Vary comic engines. Human vanity, siblings remembering an embarrassment, a parent caught by her own rule, negotiating shared space and adults admitting faults are stronger than universal talking-object jokes.
- Office and legal words are occasional character seasoning, not a house language: shared folder, appointment, qualifications, promotion, departments, professional development, conflict of interest, admissible precedent and log that separately accumulate.
- End on the consequence or relationship beat. Instructions, evidence morals and explanations after a punch routinely undo the entertainment break.
- Protect warmth by sharing fallibility. Luli can join a fantasy or admit a mistake; Mimi can be teased; Bibi can side with a child. Avoid making three generations sound like the same evidence lecturer.
- C21 is an intentional quiet repair scene after damaged evidence; comic pressure should not override its tenderness. C29 and C30 can also let sincere support breathe.
- No added all-caps comic words or typography are warranted; none currently repair a weak premise. Existing uppercase speaker labels and book headings are structural, not a comedy problem.

## Candidate review still required

A real PASS/FIX must inspect the exact root-bound candidate and record its hash. Grammatical coherence, correct mechanics and preserved turn count alone will not earn a comedy pass. The decisive questions are whether the exchange is easy to follow aloud, whether each answer actually responds, whether the comic turn lands without explanation, and whether these people feel like an affectionate family rather than a panel of instructors.
