# V03 Target Language & Style Profile — Polish

## Reader
Polish children roughly 8–12, often reading independently but also with a parent/family.

## Core feel
A modern Polish mystery/adventure book:
- clever without sounding school-like;
- funny without trying to sound “youth”;
- fast enough to keep momentum;
- clear enough that puzzle instructions never compete with prose;
- warm, dry, situational humor;
- graphic-novel energy in dialogue;
- premium book Polish, not translated interface copy.

## Natural Polish rules
Prefer:
- ordinary Polish sentence order;
- real spoken Polish in dialogue;
- concrete verbs;
- natural ellipsis;
- varied sentence length;
- idiomatic connective tissue;
- Polish punctuation and quotation conventions where the production format permits;
- concise imperatives in puzzle instructions.

Reject:
- English syntax in Polish words;
- noun-heavy “system” language unless it is deliberately an Academy system screen;
- calques such as “wykonać następny użyteczny krok” when “zrobić coś sensownego dalej” is what a Polish child would understand;
- literal joke preservation when the joke dies in Polish;
- repeated “znajduje się / zajmuje / trafia na” if a more natural puzzle-safe form works;
- corporate coaching;
- school-workshop voice;
- fake inspirational lines;
- TikTok slang or adults pretending to be 10-year-olds.

## Humor
Humor should be:
- short;
- situational;
- character-specific;
- occasionally dry/deadpan;
- understandable in Polish without explaining the joke.

A joke may be completely rebuilt if:
- its narrative function is preserved;
- no clue/fact/mechanic changes;
- the character still sounds like themselves.

## Character voice — Polish targets

### Mimi
Quick organizer, practical, slightly chaotic in her own belongings.
Polish voice: decisive, warm, quick pivots. Jokes often come from control vs. personal chaos.

### Luli
Sharp analyst, economical, dry.
Polish voice: fewer words, precise wording, strongest deadpan. Avoid over-formality.

### Dilo
Tech/code enthusiasm, confidence sometimes one step ahead of verification.
Polish voice: energetic, playful tech curiosity, occasional overconfident punchline. Never babyish.

### Nini
Observant, people-aware, brave, unexpectedly good questions.
Polish voice: direct and perceptive; humor from literal/surprising observations, not cuteness.

### Alio
Routes/recon, prepared for unlikely scenarios.
Polish voice: action-minded, practical, sometimes comically overprepared.

### Bibi
Archive mentor.
Polish voice: calm authority, dry warmth, no “wise old grandma” clichés. Can puncture nonsense in one sentence.

## Detective register
Use Polish that a child actually knows, but keep the Academy world stylish:
- dowód
- trop
- akta
- zeznanie
- współrzędne
- szyfr
- archiwum
- tablica śledcza
- werdykt / odpowiedź depending surface
- kryptonim operacyjny

Avoid adult police/legal jargon when a simpler Academy term works.

## Reader address
Gender-neutral whenever possible.
Do not force masculine/feminine forms onto the reader.

## Translationese kill-test
A segment fails if a Polish editor can plausibly guess the English sentence beneath it.
The goal is not “accurate Polish”.
The goal is **native Polish with exact source truth**.
