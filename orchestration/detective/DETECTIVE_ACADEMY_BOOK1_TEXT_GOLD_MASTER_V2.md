## MEET THE HAPPY MAKERS

The Detective Academy does not have a “normal” day.

Some mornings begin with a missing trophy. Others involve a parrot with suspiciously good timing, a backpack with the wrong owner, or Dilo announcing that a perfectly ordinary locker has “entered competitive mode.”

That is why the case table has five field detectives around it — and why it is almost never tidy.

**Mimi** turns a muddle into the next useful step.  
**Luli** reads the small print and asks what the evidence actually proves.  
**Dilo** likes codes, machines and being right slightly before the test is finished.  
**Alio** checks routes, corners and anything that might justify carrying a helmet.  
**Nini** gives people time to speak and notices what louder theories miss.  
And **Grandma Bibi**, the Academy’s archive mentor, knows where the old files are kept — and strongly prefers evidence to any question that begins with “Surely you remember…”

They solve cases together.

Usually.

There is just one problem.

At the portable certification rack beside the intake desk, five field badges hang in a neat row.

**Hook 06 is empty.**

## HOW TO INVESTIGATE

Every case follows the same rhythm, even when the puzzle changes.

1. **WHAT HAPPENED?** Read the case file like a story. Know the situation before you start moving pieces.
2. **WHAT DO YOU NEED TO PROVE?** Your Objective tells you exactly what the final answer must contain.
3. **WHAT RULES APPLY?** Read the investigation rules before touching the evidence.
4. **WHAT DOES THE TEAM NOTICE?** Happy Makers Chat gives personality, context and useful thinking — never a hidden rule.
5. **WHAT DOES THE EVIDENCE ACTUALLY SAY?** Mark facts. Cross out impossibilities. Record every final position when a map asks you to.
6. **MAKE THE CALL.** Use the response surface made for that case: circle, sort, mark, order, code or write only what the puzzle requires.
7. **CHECK THE RESULT.** Read Case Result so you know exactly what your deduction proves — and what it does not prove yet.
8. **KEEP WHAT MATTERS.** If the case tells you to save a map, code, phrase, photo or record, do it. This book has a long memory.

Stuck? Take one Hint Vault level at a time. A hint should move you forward, not steal the solve.

### CONTACT CASE RULE

In a contact case, the named person is the **anchor** — the person we can place for sure. Your job is to discover who shared their recorded room at the important moment.

That person becomes the next useful person to ask.

**Same room = possible contact. It does not mean guilty.**

## CASE WALL // WHAT TO KEEP

Your Case Wall is not a trophy shelf and definitely not a place to pin every dramatic idea Dilo has before lunch.

It is a record of things the investigation has genuinely earned.

Add only what you can point to:
- a recurring mark that really matches;
- a phrase recovered from evidence;
- a map you were told to preserve;
- a false lead you proved false;
- a rule, coordinate or code you solved yourself.

Do not fill gaps because a blank space makes you uncomfortable.

In this investigation, a blank space may be doing a job.
